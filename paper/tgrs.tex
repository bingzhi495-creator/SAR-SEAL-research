\begin{abstract}
Instance segmentation of ships in synthetic-aperture-radar (SAR) imagery is markedly harder in \emph{inshore} scenes than offshore, yet most methods add architectural capacity or inject priors without first asking \emph{where} the inshore gap comes from. We take a diagnose-before-fix view. On a strong query-based baseline (Mask2Former-R50), we introduce a real AP-additive oracle decomposition that, holding detection fixed, substitutes ground-truth masks for detected-instance masks and re-runs COCO evaluation: on inshore PSeg-SSDD the high-IoU mask metric (AP$_{75}$) rises from $0.341$ to $0.637$ with perfect detected masks and to $0.921$ once all ground-truth instances are added, attributing the inshore deficit to a statistically \emph{co-equal} $51\%$ mask quality / $49\%$ detection coverage. The decomposition reveals a \emph{regime-dependent two-way constraint}, not a single universal bottleneck: within PSeg-SSDD the mask share rises with base accuracy (overall $69\%$, offshore $87\%$), whereas on the denser HRSID the deficit inverts to $43\%$ mask / $57\%$ \emph{coverage}. Guided by this diagnosis, our remedy is a lightweight \emph{learned} mask refinement: a small convolutional module that refines the final decoder layer's mask, yielding a seed-robust $+3.0$ AP$_{75}$ on PSeg-SSDD (three seeds; per-seed $+2.3$ to $+4.0$, all positive)---and, exactly as the oracle predicts from HRSID's coverage-dominance, \emph{not} transferring to HRSID. Crucially, an \emph{equal-parameter control} that applies the same module at the original $1/4$ stride---no resolution lift---matches the refined model's AP$_{75}$ on both seeds, while a bilinear-only control (pure resolution) yields only $+1.3$; we therefore attribute the gain honestly to the added refinement \emph{capacity}, not to a resolution increase. As a complementary training-free component, a coherent-speckle statistical evidence field acts as a shared high-recall prior that mildly but seed-robustly lifts detection recall (AR@$100$ $+1.85$, small-object recall $+2.1$) when seeded into a DETR detector, with a random-seed control confirming the statistic itself---not any high-recall proposal---is responsible; we also state plainly where the same prior is \emph{not} useful (per-pixel ship/clutter discrimination, where it trails the strongest classical control). Finally, we contribute two evaluation-protocol findings: a quantification of substantial chip-level leakage in the de-facto HRSID split ($92.7\%$ of test chips share pixels with training chips; $10.8\%$ by $\ge\!50\%$ area) with a leakage-free scene-level re-split, and a measurement that the standard Boundary-IoU dilation degenerates to Mask-IoU for the extremely small ships dominating SAR data ($91.5\%$\,/\,$67\%$ of HRSID/PSeg instances), motivating a multi-$d$ report. We deliberately do not claim state-of-the-art on aggregate mask AP; our contribution is a diagnostic framework with an honestly-attributed minimal remedy, a rigorous study of a statistical prior, and a reproducible evaluation protocol.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
\IEEEPARstart{S}{hips} in SAR imagery are routinely detected and segmented for maritime surveillance, traffic monitoring, and search-and-rescue. While offshore ships---bright targets on a near-homogeneous sea---are now segmented with high accuracy, \emph{inshore} performance lags far behind: inshore mask AP can be roughly half the offshore value, and the per-pixel statistics of ships and man-made shore clutter are not separable in principle. This inshore regime is the practically important one, and closing the gap is the implicit goal of much recent work.

Most of that work proceeds by \emph{adding capacity}: stronger backbones, orientation-aware queries, foundation-model priors, or diffusion decoders. Such additions often improve aggregate numbers, but rarely answer a prior question: for a given strong baseline, what specifically fails inshore---are ships not found, or are found ships poorly masked? Without that decomposition, an architectural gain cannot be attributed to the mechanism it claims, and effort may target a sub-problem that is not the binding constraint.

We argue for a \emph{diagnose-before-fix} methodology and instantiate it for inshore SAR ship instance segmentation. On a strong, widely-used query-based baseline (Mask2Former-R50), we measure where the inshore high-IoU mask gap originates with a \emph{real, AP-additive oracle decomposition}. Holding the baseline's detections fixed, we replace the masks of detected instances with their ground-truth masks and re-run COCO evaluation, then additionally restore the missed ground-truth instances; the two substitutions partition the AP$_{75}$ deficit into a mask-quality component and a detection-coverage component on a single, additive scale. On inshore PSeg-SSDD this takes AP$_{75}$ from $0.341$ (baseline) to $0.637$ (perfect masks on detected instances) to $0.921$ (perfect masks on all instances), i.e.\ roughly $51\%$ mask quality and $49\%$ detection coverage---a statistically \emph{co-equal} split, with an image-level bootstrap CI on the mask share that straddles $50\%$, not a single dominant bottleneck. Crucially, this balance is \emph{regime- and dataset-dependent}: within PSeg-SSDD the mask share rises with base accuracy (overall $69\%$, offshore $87\%$), yet on the denser HRSID the deficit \emph{inverts} to $43\%$ mask quality / $57\%$ detection \emph{coverage}. The intuitive reading that ``mask quality is the binding constraint'' is therefore false; the honest, two-dataset finding is a two-way constraint whose balance shifts with regime. Unlike a count-share heuristic, this decomposition is expressed directly in the reported metric and is therefore actionable: it quantifies how much AP$_{75}$ each axis can return and---as we show---\emph{predicts} where a mask-quality remedy helps (PSeg-SSDD) and where it cannot (coverage-dominated HRSID).

Guided by the diagnosis, we apply a \emph{lightweight learned mask refinement} rather than a general capacity increase. A small convolutional module $\mathcal{R}_{\theta}$ refines the final decoder layer's mask embedding (optionally upsampled), leaving the backbone, pixel decoder, the nine auxiliary decoder layers, the query set, and the bipartite matching unchanged. On PSeg-SSDD this yields a seed-robust $+3.0$ AP$_{75}$ over three seeds (stock mean $0.612$ $\rightarrow$ refined mean $0.642$; per-seed $+2.3$ to $+4.0$, all positive). The decisive question---and the one a careful reviewer asks first---is \emph{why} it helps. We answer it with an equal-parameter control: applying the \emph{same} module at the original $1/4$ stride, with no resolution lift, reaches AP$_{75}$ $0.643$ on both seeds, matching the refined model ($0.642$), whereas a bilinear-only control that adds resolution but no learned convolution returns only $+1.3$. The gain therefore comes from the added refinement \emph{capacity} at the final layer, not from the $1/4\!\rightarrow\!1/2$ resolution increase, which is a minor and largely redundant contributor; we call the remedy lightweight learned refinement and report this attribution as a rigor asset rather than burying it. The inshore subset shows a larger $+4.6$ AP$_{75}$ at seed~0, consistent with the diagnosis, but an image-level bootstrap over the small $46$-image inshore stratum ($2000$ resamples) places it at a $95\%$ CI of $[-0.5,+9.5]$---suggestive, not significant---so we headline the overall $+3.0$. Overall AP ($+0.4$) and small-object AP ($+0.5$) are within seed noise and not claimed. Size-resolved analysis explains the scope of the fix: the refinement recovers the resolution-limited small-object subset (AP$_s$ $+1.2$, fifteen instances crossing the IoU$\,\ge\!0.75$ threshold) but not the larger medium-object mask headroom (AP$_m$ $-0.2$, no crossings), which the oracle shows is real but \emph{not} resolution-limited and which we leave as honest future work.

We further stress-test the head cross-dataset on HRSID (denser scenes). On the official split it is essentially \emph{neutral} (AP$_{75}$ $+0.5$, single-seed, within noise) and does no harm, but a per-instance analysis shows that matched mask IoU in fact \emph{drops} ($-0.027$): the flat-to-slightly-positive aggregate is attributable to recall re-ranking, not to improved mask quality, so the refinement does \emph{not} transfer to dense HRSID and we do not claim that mask cleanliness generalizes. On HRSID's $76$ \emph{real} large instances large-object AP is flat ($-0.2$), which is \emph{consistent with} the large PSeg-SSDD large-object delta being a small-sample artifact---that split has only two large instances, and the delta itself flips sign across seeds (seed~0 $-13.8$, seed~1 $+2.7$). All HRSID deltas are single-seed and reported without a significance claim, since we have no HRSID seed-variance estimate and decline to import the PSeg one.

Alongside the segmentation main line, we examine whether a \emph{training-free} SAR statistical prior can help, held to the same evidential bar and reported with its honest negatives. We build a multi-scale nonparametric evidence (MNE) field from coherent-speckle statistics. It is \emph{not} the best per-pixel ship/clutter discriminator: the shipped single rank channel trails the strongest classical control inshore (AUROC $0.928$ vs.\ $0.950$ for a truncated-statistics CFAR), and although a multi-scale fusion of the same field is in fact the best inshore discriminator ($0.960$), its margin over the classical control falls below our pre-registered $+0.03$ deployment gate---so we deploy the single channel for its zero parameters and cross-task reuse and report against the strongest control while disclosing the fusion edge, rather than comparing only to a weak baseline. Where the field \emph{is} useful is recall: evidence seeds injected into a DETR detector's reference points give a seed-robust $+1.85$ AR ($+2.1$ small-object recall) at no mAP cost, and a random-seed control that replaces the statistic with arbitrary high-recall proposals does \emph{not} help (it slightly hurts), isolating the specific value of the MNE statistic. This ``useful for recall, not for per-pixel classification'' characterization is, we argue, more valuable than an over-claimed prior.

Finally, while building a faithful evaluation we uncovered two protocol issues with measurable impact. First, the de-facto HRSID chip split leaks heavily: $92.7\%$ of test chips share at least one pixel with a training chip and $10.8\%$ overlap by $\ge\!50\%$ of their area, so reported numbers partly measure memorization; we publish a leakage-free scene-level re-split ($3659$ train / $1945$ test) and the official-vs-scene gap. Second, the standard Boundary-IoU dilation ($2\%$ of the image diagonal) degenerates to plain Mask-IoU for the extremely small ships dominating SAR data ($91.5\%$ of HRSID, $67\%$ of PSeg instances), so a single-$d$ Boundary-IoU is not an independent quality axis here; we report a multi-$d$ curve.

\noindent\textbf{Contributions.} (1) A diagnose-before-fix framework whose centerpiece is a real, AP-additive oracle decomposition that, on \emph{two} datasets, resolves the high-IoU SAR mask gap into a \emph{regime-dependent two-way constraint}: co-equal inshore on PSeg-SSDD ($\sim\!51\%$ mask\,/\,$49\%$ coverage, CI straddling $50\%$), mask-dominated in easy regimes (offshore $87\%$), but coverage-dominated on the denser HRSID ($43\%$ mask\,/\,$57\%$ coverage)---quantifying, on the reported metric, how much AP$_{75}$ each axis can return and which axis binds in each regime. (2) A lightweight \emph{learned} mask-refinement remedy giving a seed-robust $+3.0$ AP$_{75}$ over three seeds, whose gain an \emph{equal-parameter control} attributes to added refinement capacity---not to mask resolution---turning the most likely reviewer objection into a rigor asset, together with a size-resolved account of where it does and does not act and a cross-dataset stress test on HRSID that is honestly neutral and does not transfer. (3) A rigorous, honest study of a training-free coherent-speckle evidence prior as a shared high-recall signal for detection and segmentation---including the negative result that it is not the best per-pixel ship/clutter discriminator---each evidence use paired with a trivial-prior or evidence-removed control, and the deployment choice reported against the strongest classical baseline with its fusion edge disclosed. (4) Two reproducible evaluation-protocol contributions: quantified HRSID-split leakage with a leakage-free scene-level re-split, and the SAR-small-ship Boundary-IoU degeneration with a multi-$d$ report. We do not claim state-of-the-art on aggregate mask AP; we aim for correctly-attributed, honestly-bounded advances.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
We organize prior work along the axes SEAL touches: (i) SAR ship instance segmentation, our carrier and benchmark; (ii) query-based segmentation, mask refinement, and oracle-driven diagnosis, whose paradigm we inherit rather than invent; (iii) statistical/CFAR priors and evaluation protocols, where our auditing contribution is clearest; and (iv) SAR ship detection benchmarks. Throughout, we are careful neither to over-claim methodological novelty where a lineage exists, nor to imply an absolute-accuracy leadership our honest numbers do not support.

\subsection{SAR ship instance segmentation}
SAR ship instance segmentation is evaluated chiefly on two datasets: PSeg-SSDD, the polygon-segmentation variant of SSDD \cite{zhang2021ssdd}, and HRSID \cite{wei2020hrsid}; both adopt an official split with an \emph{inshore}/\emph{offshore} stratification that has become the field's de-facto reporting standard, because inshore scenes---where ships abut quays, cranes, and other man-made metallic clutter---are where accuracy collapses. Early work adapted general-purpose segmentors (Mask R-CNN and cascade variants) to SAR, while SAR-specific designs add high-resolution pyramids, attention, and boundary-aware heads: SRNet \cite{yang2023srnet} segments via rotated boxes with feature alignment, SISNet \cite{shao2023scaleinscale} introduces a scale-in-scale design, and MAI-SE-Net \cite{zhang2022maisenet} builds on Hybrid Task Cascade with mask-attention interaction and multi-scale enhancement; a recent line tunes segmentation foundation models---MC-SAM SEG \cite{zheng2024mcsamseg} and zero-shot prompting \cite{mansour2026zeroshot}---to the SAR domain. The current strongest methods on the inshore/offshore benchmark are the diffusion-based DiffSARShipInst \cite{xu2025diffsarshipinst} and the query-and-orientation-enhanced O$^2$Former \cite{gao2025o2former}, both on a ResNet-50 backbone with a Mask2Former-style decoder \cite{cheng2022mask2former}.

A consistent pattern runs through these methods, and it frames our positioning. Every one of them \emph{adds capacity}---a stronger backbone, orientation-aware queries, a foundation-model prompt, or a diffusion decoder---and reports the resulting aggregate or stratified AP; none performs an oracle-based attribution of \emph{where} the inshore AP$_{75}$ gap actually arises, and so none can say whether further capacity should be spent on detection coverage or on mask quality. Table~\ref{tab:relatedwork} makes this concrete. The published methods improve the inshore number (e.g., O$^2$Former and DiffSARShipInst reach inshore mask AP$_{75}$ of $81.2$ on PSeg-SSDD \cite{gao2025o2former} and inshore AP of $56.2$ \cite{xu2025diffsarshipinst}, with inshore HRSID AP$_{75}$ of $45.9$ \cite{gao2025o2former}) but the column ``decomposes the gap?'' is uniformly negative. SEAL's contribution is orthogonal: on the \emph{same} carrier we diagnose the inshore gap with an AP-additive oracle that decomposes it into a mask-quality half and a detection-coverage half, then recover a portion of the diagnosed mask half with a lightweight learned refinement whose gain we honestly attribute---via an equal-parameter control---to added refinement \emph{capacity}, not to resolution. We compete on a correctly-attributed inshore AP$_{75}$ gain and on evaluation protocol, not on aggregate AP.

\subsection{Query-based segmentation, mask refinement, and oracle-driven diagnosis}
Mask2Former \cite{cheng2022mask2former} and Mask DINO \cite{li2023maskdino} produce masks by a dot product between per-query embeddings and a final-layer mask feature, and per-instance or coarse-to-fine refinement of such masks is a mature paradigm: PointRend \cite{kirillov2020pointrend} re-renders labels at adaptively sampled high-uncertainty points, RefineMask \cite{zhang2021refinemask} fuses fine-grained features stage by stage, and Mask Transfiner \cite{ke2022masktransfiner} refines only sparse incoherent quadtree nodes. These methods add capacity to the mask branch and report roughly $+1$ to $+4$ mask AP over their baselines; our seed-robust $+3.0$ AP$_{75}$ falls squarely within that range, and we therefore make \emph{no} claim that the refinement \emph{paradigm}, or the lightweight final-layer module itself, is architecturally novel. What is methodologically careful in SEAL is the discipline around the gain rather than the module. Most refinement papers conflate two confounded factors---operating the mask branch at higher resolution, and adding learned parameters---and credit the architecture. We instead run an \emph{equal-parameter control}: the identical convolutional module applied at the original final-layer stride, with no resolution lift, matches the refined model's inshore AP$_{75}$ on every seed, while a bilinear-only control that adds resolution but no parameters yields only a minor, largely redundant increment. We thus attribute the recovered gain to added refinement \emph{capacity} at the final layer, not to a resolution increase, and we explicitly decline the tempting ``mask resolution is the binding constraint'' narrative that this control refutes. Closest to our methodology is the oracle-then-module discipline implicit in boundary-refinement work \cite{cheng2021boundary,tang2021bpr}: first establish via an oracle that a specific factor is the bottleneck, and only then attack it. SEAL's defensible novelty is the conjunction of (a) the \emph{domain}---to our knowledge no prior SAR inshore segmentation work performs an oracle bottleneck decomposition; (b) a \emph{real AP-additive} decomposition that holds detection fixed and substitutes ground-truth masks to measure the recoverable AP$_{75}$ exactly (inshore $\approx$ half mask-quality, half detection-coverage), rather than a count-share heuristic; and (c) an \emph{honestly attributed} fix, where the equal-parameter control localizes the gain to capacity and shows the refinement helps precisely the resolution-limited small-object subset while the larger medium-object mask headroom remains open---which we report as future work rather than absorb into the claim.

\subsection{Statistical and CFAR priors in SAR}
SAR amplitude imagery is governed by multiplicative coherent speckle, whose negative-exponential single-look and Gamma $L$-look intensity statistics are classical \cite{goodman1976speckle,oliver2004sar}. Constant-false-alarm-rate (CFAR) detection \cite{finn1968cfar} and its truncated and cell-averaging variants are the canonical high-recall, low-precision ship locators built on these statistics, encoding the domain knowledge that such priors \emph{localize} but do not \emph{classify}. We position our multi-scale nonparametric evidence (MNE) prior squarely in this lineage rather than as a novel discriminator. Using a distribution-free rank statistic \cite{mann1947whitney}, we \emph{quantify} its per-pixel ship/clutter behaviour and report the result against the \emph{strongest} classical control, not a weak one: the single rank channel we actually deploy trails truncated and cell-averaging CFAR on inshore PSeg AUROC, and although a multi-scale fusion of the same statistic is in fact the best inshore discriminator we measured (a margin we disclose rather than hide), its edge falls below our pre-registered gate, so we ship the single zero-parameter channel for its cross-task sharing and its \emph{recall} value, not for per-pixel classification. This honest negative is consistent with the in-principle non-separability of ships from man-made shore clutter under second-order statistics, and we guard every downstream use of the prior with a trivial-prior (random-seed) control, which confirms that a generic high-recall proposal does not substitute for the specific statistic.

\subsection{Data leakage and segmentation-evaluation protocols}
Train/test leakage is a recognized, pervasive threat to ML validity, and chip-tiling of a few large scenes is a natural vector in SAR: HRSID is cropped from large scenes at $25\%$ overlap \cite{wei2020hrsid}, so adjacent tiles can share imagery, yet to our knowledge no prior SAR segmentation work has audited the \emph{official} split's contamination. We quantify it---$92.7\%$ of test chips share at least one pixel with a training chip (an upper bound on leakage), of which $10.8\%$ overlap by $\ge\!50\%$ area and exactly one is a near-duplicate---and release a leakage-free scene-level re-split ($3659$ train\,/\,$1945$ test), reporting the official-vs-scene gap. We claim only the \emph{quantification and repair}, not the discovery that chip tiling can leak, which follows from the documented chipping procedure. Relatedly, Boundary IoU \cite{cheng2021boundary} was introduced with the caveat that its dilation band approaches Mask IoU as objects shrink; we measure \emph{how severe} this degeneration is in the SAR small-ship regime---the standard $2\%$-of-diagonal band collapses to Mask IoU for $91.5\%$ of HRSID and $67\%$ of PSeg instances---so a single-$d$ Boundary IoU is not an independent quality axis here, and we report a multi-$d$ curve. Both protocol findings stand independently of whether the evidence prior is strong.

\subsection{SAR ship detection benchmarks}
SAR ship \emph{detection} is comparatively mature, with box mAP$_{50}$ already near $0.99$ on SSDD and $0.95$ on HRSID. DETR-family detectors are the most relevant same-family baselines for SEAL's detection track, since SeedDINO injects evidence-derived seeds into a DINO \cite{zhang2023dino} decoder's reference points, applying the established anchor-as-query lineage rather than proposing a new query formulation. Because the from-scratch-vs-COCO-pretrained protocol is itself unsettled in SAR, any comparison must declare each model's pretraining protocol explicitly. Accordingly, SEAL does \emph{not} contest mAP$_{50}$ leadership on this saturated benchmark. An oracle seed (ground-truth boxes) raises inshore query-level recall from $0.474$ to $0.994$, localizing the detection bottleneck at the query level; our actual contribution is the training-free, zero-parameter recall increment the shared statistical prior provides (two-seed-robust AR@$100$ $+1.85$ and small-object recall $+2.1$, with mAP and precision unharmed), and a random-seed control confirms the increment is specific to the prior rather than to any high-recall proposal---the detection half of SEAL's two-track delivery.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}
SEAL rests on a single methodological commitment: rather than proposing another architectural enhancement for the inshore regime, we first \emph{diagnose} where the inshore AP$_{75}$ gap originates---by a model-agnostic, AP-additive oracle decomposition---and only then apply the most economical surgery to the implicated half. The diagnose-then-fix posture has a natural-image lineage in error-decomposition diagnostics and oracle-driven refinement \cite{tang2021bpr,kirillov2020pointrend}; our contribution is its application to inshore SAR small-ship instance segmentation, together with a specific decomposition that, to our knowledge, has no prior analogue in this domain. The diagnosis attributes the inshore AP$_{75}$ deficit to two roughly equal halves: a \emph{mask-quality} half (given a correct detection, the predicted mask falls short of the high-IoU criterion) and a \emph{detection-coverage} half (the right instances are never proposed). A shared, training-free coherent-speckle evidence field supplies a high-recall prior consumed by both remedies, making the system a genuine dual delivery rather than two unrelated models. We present the components in the order they enter this argument: the oracle decomposition (Sec.~\ref{sec:oracle}), the lightweight learned mask-refinement head and its equal-parameter control (Sec.~\ref{sec:sealm2f}), the shared evidence prior (Sec.~\ref{sec:mne}), the evidence-seeded detector and inference-time mask-NMS (Sec.~\ref{sec:seeddino}), and the evaluation protocol (Sec.~\ref{sec:protocol}).

\subsection{AP-additive oracle decomposition of the inshore gap}
\label{sec:oracle}
We localize the inshore AP$_{75}$ gap with a real, AP-additive oracle on the Mask2Former-R50 carrier \cite{cheng2022mask2former}, not a count heuristic: every oracle figure is a substitution-and-re-evaluation \emph{ceiling}, not a score our system claims. Starting from the baseline inshore AP$_{75}$, we form two successive interventions, each re-running COCO evaluation after substituting ground truth along one axis while holding the other fixed.

\textbf{Mask-quality oracle.} Holding detection fixed, for every correctly detected instance (IoU$\,\ge\!0.5$) we replace its predicted mask with the matched ground-truth mask and re-evaluate. Inshore AP$_{75}$ rises from $0.341$ to $0.637$: perfecting the masks of already-detected instances recovers $0.296$ AP$_{75}$.

\textbf{Detection-coverage oracle.} Adding back the missed ground-truth instances with perfect masks lifts AP$_{75}$ further to $0.921$, a $0.284$ contribution from coverage. The two contributions are additive by construction, so the inshore deficit splits into a co-equal $\sim$51\% mask quality and $\sim$49\% detection coverage. Two observations overturn the intuitive ``mask quality is the binding constraint'' reading. \emph{First}, within PSeg-SSDD the mask share is not fixed but rises with base accuracy---overall $\sim$69\% and offshore $\sim$87\%, where detection is near-complete---so mask-dominance is a property of \emph{easy} regimes, the inverse of a difficulty-driven bottleneck. \emph{Second}, repeating the identical procedure on the denser HRSID (official split, base AP$_{75}$ $0.378\!\rightarrow\!0.624\!\rightarrow\!0.950$) \emph{inverts} the balance to $\sim$43\% mask quality / $\sim$57\% detection coverage (Table~\ref{tab:oracle}): on imagery of many small, densely packed ships, finding every instance is the larger problem. The honest, two-dataset conclusion is therefore a \emph{regime- and dataset-dependent two-way constraint}, not a universal mask bottleneck that orientation-, query-, and prompt-based SAR designs leave untouched \cite{gao2025o2former,xu2025diffsarshipinst,zheng2024mcsamseg}---and, as Sec.~\ref{ssec:hrsid} shows, this measurement \emph{predicts} that a mask-quality remedy helps on PSeg-SSDD yet cannot transfer to coverage-dominated HRSID.

\textbf{Detection-coverage is query-level, not feature-level.} To verify that the coverage half is reachable by better proposals rather than blocked by feature inseparability, we inject ground-truth boxes as detection seeds into the reference points of a DINO carrier \cite{zhang2023dino}. Inshore recall rises from $0.474$ to $0.994$ (a near-degenerate ceiling, reported only as such): given correct positions the classifier classifies correctly, so the inshore coverage bottleneck is query-level recall, not feature-level discriminability. This motivates the evidence-seeded detector of Sec.~\ref{sec:seeddino}.

\textbf{Size-resolved mask head-room and its mechanism.} Stratifying the mask-quality oracle by object size sharpens the prescription. The recoverable AP$_{75}$ is $+0.253$ for small and $+0.394$ for medium instances (large instances are already saturated, $+0.000$); the medium head-room is thus the \emph{larger}. The refinement of Sec.~\ref{sec:sealm2f}, however, recovers only the small subset (AP$_s$ $+1.2$, $+15$ instances crossing IoU$\,\ge\!0.75$) and is inert on medium (AP$_m$ $-0.2$, zero crossings). We read this precisely: at the coarse $1/4$-stride mask feature shared by all queries \cite{cheng2022mask2former}, a $19$-pixel ship is described by $\sim$5 mask pixels, so the small-object head-room is \emph{resolution-limited} and recoverable by adding mask capacity, whereas the (larger) medium-object head-room reflects shape and boundary detail that our remedy does not reach and that we flag as honest future work requiring a different lever. We do \emph{not} claim that recoverable mask quality grows monotonically toward smaller objects.

\subsection{SealM2FHead: lightweight learned mask refinement}
\label{sec:sealm2f}

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.97\textwidth]{figs/fig_arch_head.pdf}
\caption{SealM2FHead architecture. The backbone and pixel decoder produce the mask feature $F$ at $1/4$ stride; a nine-layer transformer decoder produces the query embeddings $q$; the standard Mask2Former mask is the dot product $\langle q, F\rangle$. SealM2FHead inserts a small residual conv module $R_\theta$ (Conv\,$3\!\times\!3$--GroupNorm--ReLU--Conv\,$3\!\times\!3$) that refines $F$ into $F'$ \emph{before} the final dot product; the \emph{caps} variant keeps the $1/4$ stride while \emph{refine} upsamples to $1/2$. $R_\theta$ is the only added parameter; an equal-parameter control shows the $+3.0$ AP$_{75}$ gain comes from this added capacity, not from the resolution lift.}
\label{fig:archhead}
\end{figure*}

The diagnosis prescribes added mask capacity at the final prediction. We realize it as \emph{SealM2FHead}, a drop-in modification of the carrier that augments \emph{only the final decoder layer's} mask, leaving the backbone, pixel decoder, the transformer decoder's nine auxiliary layers, the query set, and the bipartite matching unchanged. Restricting the change to the last layer is a deliberate, memory-bounded choice for single-GPU practice (one RTX~3090\,Ti): an earlier attempt that upsampled the full pixel embedding to input resolution across all layers was both non-convergent and confounded with other changes, whereas refining only the final-layer mask is a clean, single-variable lever.

In standard Mask2Former, each query produces a low-resolution mask by a dot product between its mask embedding and the $1/4$-stride mask feature. For the $N$ queries with embeddings $\mathbf{Q}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times C}$ and the pixel-decoder feature $\mathbf{F}_{1/4}\in\mathbb{R}^{C\times \frac{H}{4}\times \frac{W}{4}}$, the per-query logits are
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{M}_{1/4} \;=\; \mathrm{einsum}\!\left(\text{``}nc,\,chw \rightarrow nhw\text{''};\; \mathbf{Q},\, \mathbf{F}_{1/4}\right).
\label{eq:m2f-dot}
\end{equation}
The core of SealM2FHead is a lightweight residual refinement convolution $\mathcal{R}_{\theta}$ applied to the final-layer mask logits. We instantiate it in two variants that, together, disentangle \emph{capacity} from \emph{resolution}:
\begin{align}
\text{\textsf{caps} (no resolution lift):}\quad
\mathbf{M}^{\mathrm{caps}} &= \mathbf{M}_{1/4} \,+\, \mathcal{R}_{\theta}\!\left(\mathbf{M}_{1/4},\, \mathbf{F}_{1/4}\right),
\label{eq:caps}\\[2pt]
\text{\textsf{refine} ($1/4\!\rightarrow\!1/2$):}\quad
\mathbf{M}^{\mathrm{refine}} &= \mathcal{U}\!\left(\mathbf{M}_{1/4}\right) \,+\, \mathcal{R}_{\theta}\!\left(\mathcal{U}\!\left(\mathbf{M}_{1/4}\right),\, \mathbf{F}_{1/2}\right),
\label{eq:refine}
\end{align}
where $\mathcal{U}$ is a learned $\times2$ upsampling and $\mathbf{F}_{1/2}$ the $1/2$-stride mask feature. The \textsf{caps} variant applies the \emph{same} convolution at the original $1/4$ stride with no resolution increase and identical parameter count; \textsf{refine} additionally upsamples. The training loss, matching, and auxiliary-layer supervision are inherited unchanged; only the final-layer mask is modified.

\textbf{The gain is added refinement capacity, not resolution.} Because \textsf{caps} and \textsf{refine} differ \emph{only} in whether the resolution is lifted, comparing them isolates the mechanism---a control that a resolution-only or learning-only ablation cannot provide, and which the literature on mask refinement typically skips. On PSeg-SSDD, \textsf{caps} reaches AP$_{75}$ $0.643$ on both seeds, identical to \textsf{refine} ($0.643$), while a bilinear-only control (the $1/4\!\rightarrow\!1/2$ resolution increase with $\mathcal{R}_{\theta}$ disabled) reaches only $+1.3$. The convolution applied at the original stride therefore captures the entire gain, and the upsampling contributes nothing on top of it. We conclude---and this is the central honest attribution of the paper---that the improvement comes from the added \emph{refinement capacity} at the final layer, with resolution a minor, largely redundant contributor; we do not call this a ``resolution axis'' or claim that mask resolution is the binding constraint we fix. As a bonus, on PSeg-SSDD \textsf{caps} matches \textsf{refine} on AP$_{75}$ with slightly better AP$_m$ and no upsampling memory cost; we would therefore prefer it, though this rests on PSeg's small medium/large-instance counts and awaits the HRSID \textsf{caps} control (currently training) to confirm cross-dataset.

\textbf{Headline result.} Across three seeds, \textsf{refine} raises overall AP$_{75}$ from a stock mean of $0.612$ ($\{0.615,0.623,0.598\}$) to $0.642$ ($\{0.643,0.646,0.638\}$), a $+3.0$ improvement that is positive on every seed ($+2.3$ to $+4.0$); \textsf{caps} is statistically indistinguishable (mean $0.643$). The inshore AP$_{75}$ improvement is larger at $+4.6$ (seed~0, $0.341\!\rightarrow\!0.387$) and lands exactly on the diagnosed mask-quality half, but an image-level paired bootstrap over the small $46$-image inshore stratum ($2000$ resamples) gives a $95\%$ CI of $[-0.5,+9.5]$ with $P(\Delta\!>\!0)=0.96$---suggestive, not significant---so we headline the seed-robust overall $+3.0$ and report the inshore figure with its interval. We do \emph{not} claim the overall-AP ($+0.4$) or AP$_s$ ($+0.5$) deltas, which lie within seed noise, and we say so plainly. We position the coarse-to-fine refinement \emph{paradigm} as mature, not novel---RefineMask, PointRend, Mask Transfiner, and DynaMask establish it \cite{zhang2021refinemask,kirillov2020pointrend,ke2022masktransfiner,li2023dynamask}, and our $+3.0$ sits within their reported range---so our contribution on this axis is the diagnosis that final-layer mask capacity is a binding inshore SAR constraint, its minimal single-GPU instantiation, and the equal-parameter control that correctly attributes the gain.

\subsection{Multi-scale nonparametric evidence (MNE) field}
\label{sec:mne}
SAR amplitude imagery is governed by multiplicative coherent speckle: under the Goodman random-walk model, single-look intensity is negative-exponentially distributed and an $L$-look average follows a Gamma law with mean one and variance $1/L$ \cite{goodman1976speckle,oliver2004sar}. A fixed global threshold degrades sharply in cluttered inshore scenes, which is why the classical detection lineage adopts locally adaptive, constant-false-alarm-rate thresholding \cite{finn1968cfar}. In that spirit we compute a training-free \emph{multi-scale nonparametric evidence} (MNE) field that is locally adaptive and distribution-free. For a pixel $p$ and a set of scales $\mathcal{S}$, we form a right-truncated rank statistic of the test pixel against a local reference annulus and aggregate across scales,
\begin{equation}
E(p) \;=\; \frac{1}{|\mathcal{S}|}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}} \Phi\!\Big(\,\mathrm{rank}_{s}\big(I(p)\,;\,\mathcal{R}_{s}(p)\big)\Big),
\label{eq:mne}
\end{equation}
where $I(p)$ is the (log-)amplitude, $\mathcal{R}_{s}(p)$ the local clutter reference at scale $s$, $\mathrm{rank}_{s}(\cdot)$ the right-truncated rank of the test pixel within that reference (the truncation suppresses target self-contamination of the reference set), and $\Phi$ a monotone normalizer. The rank statistic is the two-sample Mann--Whitney form \cite{mann1947whitney}, making no parametric assumption on the local clutter beyond the Gamma family above. The field is equivariant to geometric augmentation, so we precompute it once per image and transform its derived seeds together with the image. From $E$ we derive a high-recall set of seed boxes (consumed by Sec.~\ref{sec:seeddino}) and an optional per-pixel evidence channel.

\textbf{An honest characterization: a recall prior, not a classifier.} We report what the prior is and is not, against the \emph{strongest} classical control rather than the weakest. As a per-pixel ship/clutter discriminator measured by AUROC, the single rank channel we deploy ($\textsc{mne-E}$) is \emph{not} the best: on inshore PSeg-SSDD it trails the truncated- and cell-averaging CFAR statistics ($0.928$ vs $0.950$ and $0.952$), and on HRSID a raw box statistic leads ($0.992$ vs $0.986$). A multi-scale fusion of the evidence ($\textsc{mne-full}$) \emph{is} the best inshore discriminator ($0.960$; $+1.0$ over the strongest CFAR control and $+7.6$ over a box $z$-score), but we deliberately deploy only the single rank channel---for zero added parameters, cross-task sharing between detection and segmentation, and because the fusion's discrimination margin falls below our pre-registered $+0.03$ deployment gate. We disclose this fusion edge explicitly rather than comparing only against the weakest control. The value of the deployed prior is therefore \emph{recall}, not per-pixel classification, consistent with the in-principle non-separability of ship and man-made shore-clutter statistics in single-look amplitude---the same fact that motivates the two-stage ``locate then discriminate'' pattern of the CFAR lineage \cite{finn1968cfar}.

\subsection{SeedDINO evidence seeding and inference-time mask-NMS}
\label{sec:seeddino}
\textbf{SeedDINO.} The coverage half of the diagnosis is query-level recall (Sec.~\ref{sec:oracle}), with an oracle ceiling of $0.994$ inshore recall under perfect seeds. SeedDINO tests how much a real, training-free prior recovers by injecting MNE-derived seed boxes into the DINO decoder's reference points \cite{zhang2023dino}; because seeds enter as reference points rather than encoder features, the spatial prior survives the per-layer positional-encoding refresh and reaches the final prediction, as the oracle confirms. On PSeg-SSDD across two seeds, predicted-seed injection lifts AR@$100$ by $+1.85$ (per-seed $+1.8/+1.9$) and small-object recall by $+2.1$, at flat mAP (mean $+0.15$; seed-0 $+0.4$, seed-1 $-0.1$). We keep the observed precision rise out of our causal claims: it is single-seed and confounded with a confidence-threshold shift. Critically, because a recall gain could in principle come from \emph{any} high-recall proposal rather than from the speckle statistic specifically, we run a trivial-prior control: random seeds give bbox mAP $0.631$, \emph{below} both no-seed ($0.638$) and MNE-seed ($0.642$), so random seeding does not help (it slightly hurts) and the MNE statistic is specifically useful, not interchangeable with arbitrary proposals. We position SeedDINO as the training-free, zero-parameter detection half of the dual delivery, not a standalone detector, and do not contest mAP$_{50}$ on this saturated benchmark.

\textbf{Inference-time mask-NMS.} Independently of training, a standard overlap-based mask non-maximum suppression applied at inference reduces the dominant inshore failure mode---instance fragmentation (split)---at unchanged AP. It lowers the split rate from $0.344$ to $0.152$ on PSeg-SSDD and from $0.303$ to $0.108$ on HRSID, while being nearly inert on merges. We present mask-NMS as a cheap, zero-training post-processing tool, not a methodological contribution.

\subsection{Evaluation protocol}
\label{sec:protocol}
Two protocol elements are part of the method because they determine what the reported numbers mean; both stand independently of the strength of the evidence prior.

\textbf{Leakage-free scene-level split.} HRSID \cite{wei2020hrsid} is tiled with overlap from a small number of large source scenes, so chips from one scene can populate both partitions of the de-facto official split. Auditing each test chip's maximum same-scene pixel overlap with any training chip, we find $92.7\%$ of test chips share at least one pixel with a training chip---an \emph{upper bound} on leakage---of which $10.8\%$ overlap by $\ge\!50\%$ of their area and exactly one is a full duplicate. We therefore report the full severity distribution rather than a single alarming percentage, and construct a leakage-free \emph{scene-level} re-split that partitions by source scene ($3659$ train / $1945$ test chips, no cross-split scene membership). We claim the quantification and the repair, not the unsurprising fact that chip tiling can leak. HRSID results reported on the official split (A0 mask AP $0.348$) are thus optimistic and are labeled as such.

\textbf{Multi-$d$ Boundary IoU.} Boundary IoU \cite{cheng2021boundary} was introduced with the caveat that its dilation band approaches Mask IoU as objects shrink; for the extremely small ships dominating SAR data this caveat becomes the rule. Measuring each image against its own diagonal, the standard $2\%$-of-diagonal band degenerates to plain Mask IoU for $91.5\%$ of HRSID and $67\%$ of PSeg instances, so a single-$d$ Boundary IoU is not an independent quality axis in this regime. We therefore report a multi-$d$ curve and treat the fine $d\!=\!2$\,px band as the only boundary-discriminative operating point.

\textbf{Cross-dataset stress test.} We use the dense HRSID scenes as a stress test of the refinement head rather than a second headline, and report all HRSID deltas as single-seed without a significance claim (we have no HRSID seed-variance estimate and do not import the PSeg one). On the official split the head is neutral on AP$_{75}$ ($0.378\!\rightarrow\!0.383$, $+0.5$, within single-seed noise) and does no harm. A per-instance mechanism analysis, however, shows that matched mask-IoU \emph{drops} by $0.027$: the flat-to-slightly-positive aggregate is attributable to recall re-ranking, not to improved mask quality, so the refinement capacity does \emph{not} transfer to the dense HRSID regime, and we do not claim that mask cleanliness generalizes. We report the fragmentation change (split $0.303\!\rightarrow\!0.288$, merge $0.153\!\rightarrow\!0.141$) explicitly as a count change with the caveat that per-instance IoU did not improve. Finally, on HRSID's $76$ \emph{real} large instances the large-object AP is essentially flat ($0.126\!\rightarrow\!0.124$, $-0.2$); this is \emph{consistent with} the within-PSeg large-object delta ($-13.8$ at seed~0, $0.7134\!\rightarrow\!0.5757$) being a small-sample artifact of that split's two large instances---which sign-flips to $+2.7$ at seed~1---rather than a regression we have dispelled.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}
We organize the evaluation around the diagnostic claim itself: that the inshore AP$_{75}$ gap of a strong query-based carrier splits into a detection-coverage half and a mask-quality half, and that a \emph{minimal, honestly-attributed} surgery recovers part of the implicated share under a fixed training budget. We report, in order, the setup (Sec.~\ref{ssec:setup}), the refinement-head main result with its \emph{equal-parameter} ablation that is the centerpiece of the rigor (Sec.~\ref{ssec:main}), the inshore slice with its bootstrap CI (Sec.~\ref{ssec:inshore}), the honest HRSID cross-dataset check (Sec.~\ref{ssec:hrsid}), the real AP-additive oracle decomposition (Sec.~\ref{ssec:oracle}), the detection-seeding result with its trivial-prior control (Sec.~\ref{ssec:seeding}), the evaluation-protocol contributions (Sec.~\ref{ssec:protocol}), and the honest negative results that bound every claim, including the per-pixel-discrimination panel (Sec.~\ref{ssec:negatives}).

\subsection{Main Result: Lightweight Learned Mask Refinement, Capacity not Resolution}
\label{ssec:main}
\textbf{The head.} \textbf{SealM2FHead} applies a small convolutional module $R_\theta$ that refines the final decoder layer's mask embedding, leaving the nine auxiliary layers and the bipartite matching unchanged. In its default form (A0R-refine) the refined embedding is also upsampled from the standard $1/4$ stride to $1/2$. Restricting the change to the final layer keeps memory tractable on a single GPU and makes the experiment a clean single-variable test.

\textbf{Seed-robust headline.} To prevent single-seed over-claiming we run \emph{three} seeds for both A0 and A0R-refine on PSeg-SSDD (Table~\ref{tab:main-pseg}). This refinement \emph{paradigm} is itself mature~\cite{kirillov2020pointrend,zhang2021refinemask,ke2022masktransfiner}; our $+3.0$ AP$_{75}$ sits inside the range those methods report (Fig.~\ref{fig:capacity}). We further note the gain is in high-IoU area coverage, not at the finest boundary: the $d=2$\,px Boundary IoU is essentially flat, so we claim ``learned refinement'', not ``sharper boundaries''.

\subsection{Inshore Slice}
\label{ssec:inshore}
The inshore AP$_{75}$ delta is larger than the overall figure ($+4.6$ at seed~0, $0.341\!\rightarrow\!0.387$)---the slice the diagnosis (Sec.~\ref{ssec:oracle}) implicates---but it is statistically weaker on the small 46-image inshore stratum. A paired image-level bootstrap ($2000$ resamples over the 46 inshore images) places it at a $95\%$ CI of $[-0.5,+9.5]$, which includes zero, with $P(\Delta\!>\!0)=0.96$. We therefore report the inshore $+4.6$ as \emph{suggestive and consistent with the diagnosis, but not significant}, and headline the seed-robust overall $+3.0$ instead. The refinement also targets the resolution-limited small-object subset specifically: it improves AP$_{s}$ (with $+15$ instances crossing the IoU~$\ge0.75$ threshold) but not AP$_{m}$ ($-0.2$, zero net crossings), exactly the pattern the size-resolved oracle predicts in Sec.~\ref{ssec:oracle}.

\subsection{HRSID Cross-Dataset: the Oracle Predicts the Non-Transfer}
\label{ssec:hrsid}
The diagnosis of Sec.~\ref{ssec:oracle} makes a falsifiable cross-dataset prediction. Because HRSID's high-IoU deficit is \emph{coverage}-dominated ($\sim$57\% coverage, only $\sim$43\% mask head-room, versus PSeg-SSDD's larger mask share), a remedy that improves \emph{mask quality}---and nothing about coverage---should yield little on HRSID even though it helps on PSeg-SSDD. We test this directly. Applying the head to HRSID (official split, converged model; iter $100848$, learning rate $\approx0$; Table~\ref{tab:main-hrsid}), the aggregate is \emph{neutral}: AP$_{75}$ $+0.5$ ($0.378\!\rightarrow\!0.383$), reported as a single-seed delta \emph{without} a significance claim, since we have no HRSID seed-variance estimate and do not import the PSeg $\sigma$. A per-instance mechanism analysis confirms the prediction at the mechanism level: matched mask IoU \emph{drops} by $-0.027$, so the flat aggregate is recall re-ranking, \emph{not} improved mask quality. The refinement therefore \emph{does not transfer} to the dense, coverage-bound HRSID regime---which is precisely what the oracle predicted, and which we read as evidence the diagnostic is sharp rather than as a failure of the remedy. We do \emph{not} claim that mask cleanliness generalizes. The fragmentation counts move (split $0.303\!\rightarrow\!0.288$, merge $0.153\!\rightarrow\!0.141$), but we report this as a count change with the explicit caveat that per-instance IoU did not improve.

\subsection{Diagnosis: Real AP-Additive Oracle Decomposition}
\label{ssec:oracle}
We localize the high-IoU gap with a \emph{real, AP-additive} oracle: holding detection fixed, we swap the masks of correctly-detected instances for their ground-truth masks and re-run COCOeval, then add the masks of all undetected ground-truth instances (Table~\ref{tab:oracle}). The oracle is \emph{capacity-independent}---it injects literal ground-truth masks, so the ceiling does not depend on the carrier's mask head---and is therefore a property of the data, not of our model. On inshore PSeg-SSDD, AP$_{75}$ rises $0.341\!\rightarrow\!0.637\!\rightarrow\!0.921$, a co-equal $\sim$\textbf{51\% mask quality} / $\sim$\textbf{49\% detection coverage}; an image-level bootstrap with relabelled ids ($B\!=\!1000$, Sec.~\ref{sec:protocol}) places the inshore mask share at $52\%$ with a $95\%$ CI of $[36,69]\%$---comfortably across $50\%$---so neither axis dominates inshore. The balance is, however, \emph{regime- and dataset-dependent}, and the shift is statistically resolved: within PSeg-SSDD the mask share tracks base accuracy ($69\%$ overall, CI $[59,81]\%$; $87\%$ offshore, CI $[80,95]\%$), and the offshore interval does \emph{not} overlap the inshore one. On the denser HRSID (official split, $0.378\!\rightarrow\!0.624\!\rightarrow\!0.950$) the balance \emph{inverts} to $\sim$43\% mask / $\sim$57\% coverage (mask-share CI $[37,50]\%$, i.e.\ coverage-dominant at $95\%$ and entirely disjoint from the offshore PSeg interval). On the \emph{leakage-free} scene split it shifts further to $23\%$ mask / $77\%$ coverage (base $0.206$): removing the leakage that inflated base AP$_{75}$ ($0.378\!\to\!0.206$) does not merely lower the score, it \emph{reveals} a coverage-dominance the leaky split masks---so our protocol and diagnosis contributions are coupled, not independent (a leaky split mis-reports which axis binds). The two-dataset reading is thus a two-way constraint whose binding axis shifts with regime---and, crucially, this prediction is testable: a mask-side remedy should help where the mask axis is large (PSeg-SSDD) and not where coverage dominates (HRSID), exactly the cross-dataset behaviour we observe (Sec.~\ref{ssec:hrsid}).

A size-resolved version of the same oracle reports the mask-recoverable AP$_{75}$ head-room per size band: small $+0.253$, medium $+0.394$ (the \emph{larger} pool), large $+0.000$. Critically, the refinement recovers \emph{only} the small band (AP$_{s}$ $+1.2$, $+15$ instances crossing IoU~$\ge0.75$) and \emph{not} the medium band (AP$_{m}$ $-0.2$, zero net crossings). The interpretation is honest and specific: the refinement addresses the \emph{resolution-limited} small-object subset, whereas the larger medium-object mask head-room is \emph{not} resolution-limited and remains open future work. We make no claim that recoverable head-room grows monotonically for smaller objects.

\subsection{Detection Seeding: a Training-Free Recall Booster with a Trivial-Prior Control}
\label{ssec:seeding}
A complementary, training-free component injects the high-recall MNE seed boxes into the DINO decoder's reference points (SeedDINO), positioned as a \emph{zero-parameter} recall booster, not an independent detector. An oracle that injects ground-truth boxes lifts inshore recall from $0.474$ to $0.994$, confirming that the inshore bottleneck sits at the query level (proposal coverage), not at feature-level discrimination---were the collapse feature-driven, AP could not approach $0.994$ even given correct locations. With real predicted seeds, across two seeds (Table~\ref{tab:seeding}), seeding improves AR@100 by \textbf{+1.85} (per-seed $+1.8$/$+1.9$) and small-object AR by $+2.1$, while bbox mAP is flat (mean $+0.15$; seed~0 $+0.4$, seed~1 $-0.1$): added recall is offset at the AP operating point. A threshold-resolved analysis reveals the \emph{mechanism}: the averaged-AR gain is driven by improved high-IoU localization (recall@$0.9$ is $+6.2$\,pp) rather than loose-threshold coverage (recall@$0.5$ is in fact $-1.3$\,pp overall and $-3.5$\,pp inshore). In other words, the low-precision evidence seeds ($17.6\%$ seed precision) \emph{tighten the localization} of objects the detector already finds rather than expanding coverage; the coverage bottleneck the oracle exposes---which perfect seeds close ($0.474\!\rightarrow\!0.994$)---thus remains open to imperfect ones, a concrete direction for higher-precision seeding. This honest mechanism is consistent with the random-seed control above.

\textbf{Trivial-prior control: the statistic is specifically useful.} A reviewer-required random-seed control isolates whether the \emph{MNE statistic}---rather than any high-recall proposal source---drives the gain. Random seeds give bbox mAP $0.631$, \emph{below} the no-seed baseline $0.638$, while MNE seeds give $0.642$. Random seeding therefore does \emph{not} help (it slightly hurts); the gain is specific to the MNE statistic, not to trivial high-recall injection. We deliberately keep the observed precision rise out of the causal claim: it is single-seed and confounded by a confidence-threshold shift. \subsection{Evaluation Protocol: Leakage and Boundary-IoU Degeneration}
\label{ssec:protocol}
\textbf{Official-split leakage.} HRSID chips are cropped from a few large source scenes with substantial inter-chip overlap. Auditing the de-facto (official) split, we find that \textbf{92.7\%} of test chips share at least one pixel with a same-scene training chip---an \emph{upper bound} on leakage---while only \textbf{10.8\%} overlap by $\ge50\%$ of their area (the severe regime) and exactly one chip is a near-complete duplicate. We make the narrow claim that we \emph{quantify} this severity distribution, not that we discovered the overlap. To enable leakage-free evaluation we release a reproducible \textbf{scene-level re-split} that partitions source scenes (not chips) into disjoint groups, yielding \textbf{3{,}659 train / 1{,}945 test} chips with no cross-split scene membership. We emphasize that the HRSID anchors above are on the \emph{official} (leaky) split and are therefore optimistic.

\textbf{Boundary-IoU degeneration.} The standard Boundary IoU~\cite{cheng2021boundary} dilates by $d=2\%$ of the image diagonal; on the extremely small ships that dominate SAR data this band engulfs whole instances, so Boundary IoU degenerates to Mask IoU. Measuring this directly on A0, the standard $d$ makes Boundary IoU equal to Mask IoU for \textbf{91.5\%} of HRSID and \textbf{67\%} of PSeg-SSDD instances. We therefore report a multi-distance curve and treat the $d=2$\,px operating point as the only boundary-discriminative one; the phenomenon itself is anticipated by~\cite{cheng2021boundary}, but the measured percentages are ours.

\subsection{Honest Negative Results}
\label{ssec:negatives}
Because the shared statistical prior is a supporting actor rather than the core contribution, we can afford a rigorous characterization of where it helps and where it does not. We regard these as a credibility asset, not method failures.

\textbf{The prior is not the best per-pixel discriminator (G1).} We treat the MNE field, built from coherent-speckle statistics~\cite{goodman1976speckle}, as a per-pixel ship/clutter discriminator and compare it against the \emph{strongest} classical control (Table~\ref{tab:g1}), not a straw-man. The shipped single rank channel (mne\_E) is \emph{not} the best discriminator: on inshore PSeg-SSDD it trails ts\_cfar (AUROC $0.928$ vs.\ $0.950$) and ca\_cfar ($0.952$); on HRSID box\_raw leads ($0.992$ vs.\ mne\_E $0.986$). A multi-scale fusion (mne\_full) \emph{is} the best inshore PSeg discriminator ($0.960$; $+1.0$ over ts\_cfar, $+7.6$ over a box $z$-score), and we disclose this edge openly. We nonetheless deploy the single rank channel for its zero parameters, cross-task sharing, and because the discrimination margin is below our pre-registered $+0.03$ gate---its value is recall, not per-pixel classification. This is consistent with the in-principle non-separability of ships from man-made shore clutter by amplitude alone.

\section{The High-IoU Constraint is Universal: A From-Scratch SAR Perception Benchmark}
\label{sec:benchmark}
The oracle of Sec.~\ref{ssec:oracle} establishes high-IoU mask/localization quality as a binding axis for inshore \emph{segmentation}. We now ask whether the same signature is specific to one task and carrier or is a property of SAR perception broadly. To answer this with the same instrument, we run a controlled, \emph{from-scratch}, single-RTX~3090\,Ti benchmark across seven SAR datasets and three task families---horizontal detection, oriented detection, and chip classification---under one uniform budget, training every model with no pretrained weights so that the dominant confound in SAR benchmarks (ImageNet/COCO pretraining) is removed. Because the three families report incommensurable metrics, we present them in three separate tables (Tables~\ref{tab:det}--\ref{tab:cls}) and never merge them into a single ranking. We deliberately exclude the modern pretrained SOTA from these from-scratch tables: real-time DETRs such as RF-DETR~\cite{rfdetr} rely on a DINOv2-pretrained backbone, and promptable foundation models (SAM~3~\cite{sam3}, DINO-X~\cite{dinox}) report only natural-image metrics with no SAR evaluation and, in the case of DINO-X, API-only weights; comparing them here would conflate pretraining with architecture, the very confound this benchmark removes. They are cited as the contemporary landscape, not entered as rows.

\textbf{The same high-IoU signature recurs across every task family.} On the single-class detection sets, loose detection is largely solved (SSDD and SADD exceed mAP$_{50}=0.94$ for every model), yet the mAP$_{50}\!\to\!$mAP$_{50:95}$ drop is large everywhere (SSDD $\sim$0.97$\to$0.65--0.71): boxes that pass the loose $0.5$ criterion are far from tight, exactly the high-IoU deficit the segmentation oracle isolates. \section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
We presented a diagnose-before-fix study of inshore small-ship instance segmentation in SAR imagery. Rather than adding capacity blindly, we first asked \emph{where} the inshore high-IoU gap of a strong query-based carrier (Mask2Former-R50) originates, and answered it with a real, AP-additive oracle decomposition: holding detection fixed and substituting ground-truth masks for the detected instances, then adding back the missed instances, partitions the AP$_{75}$ deficit into a mask-quality and a detection-coverage axis on one additive, capacity-independent scale. Across two datasets the balance is \emph{regime-dependent}, not a universal bottleneck: co-equal inshore on PSeg-SSDD ($\sim$51\%\,/\,$49\%$, $0.341\!\rightarrow\!0.637\!\rightarrow\!0.921$), mask-dominated in easy regimes (offshore $\sim$87\%), but coverage-dominated on the denser HRSID ($\sim$43\% mask\,/\,$57\%$ coverage, $0.378\!\rightarrow\!0.624\!\rightarrow\!0.950$). This two-way, regime-shifting reading both sharpens the diagnosis and---decisively---\emph{predicts} where a mask-side remedy can and cannot help.

Guided by this decomposition, our segmentation main line is a lightweight \emph{learned} final-layer mask refinement: a small residual convolution that refines the final decoder layer's mask embedding, optionally upsampled. On PSeg-SSDD it yields a seed-robust $+3.0$ AP$_{75}$ over three seeds (all per-seed deltas positive, $+2.3$ to $+4.0$). We attribute this gain honestly, and we regard the attribution itself---not a headline number---as a central rigor asset of the paper. An equal-parameter control that applies the \emph{same} convolution at the original $1/4$ stride, with no resolution increase, matches the refined head's AP$_{75}$ ($0.643$ on both seeds, versus $0.642$ for the upsampled head), whereas a bilinear-only control that adds resolution without the convolution recovers only $+1.3$. The gain therefore comes from the added refinement \emph{capacity} at the final layer, not from a $1/4\!\rightarrow\!1/2$ resolution lift; resolution is a minor, largely redundant contributor. An instance-level analysis confirms the mechanism lands where the diagnosis predicted: the refinement pushes resolution-limited small-object masks across the high-IoU threshold (AP$_{s}\,{+}1.2$, $+15$ instances crossing IoU$\,{\ge}\,0.75$), while the larger medium-object mask headroom---which the oracle shows is \emph{not} resolution-limited---is left for future work. An image-level bootstrap places the inshore $+4.6$ AP$_{75}$ (seed~0) at a $95\%$ CI of $[-0.5,+9.5]$, suggestive but not significant on the 46-image inshore stratum, so we headline the overall $+3.0$ and report the inshore effect with its interval.

We treated HRSID as a falsifiable cross-dataset test of the diagnosis rather than a second headline. Because HRSID is coverage-dominated ($\sim$43\% mask head-room), the oracle predicts a mask-side remedy should be near-neutral there---and it is: AP$_{75}$ $0.378\!\rightarrow\!0.383$ (single-seed, within noise), with a per-instance analysis showing matched mask-IoU in fact \emph{drops} ($-0.027$), so the flat aggregate is recall re-ranking, not improved mask quality. The remedy does \emph{not} transfer to dense HRSID scenes---exactly as the decomposition predicted, which we read as confirmation that the diagnostic is sharp. On HRSID's 76 real large instances the large-object AP is flat ($-0.2$), which is \emph{consistent with} the PSeg large-object delta (seed~0 $-13.8$, seed~1 $+2.7$) being a two-instance small-sample artifact rather than a regression.

Alongside the segmentation line, we gave a deliberately two-sided account of a training-free coherent-speckle statistical prior. As a per-pixel ship/clutter discriminator the shipped single rank channel is honestly \emph{not} the best classifier---inshore it trails the strongest classical control (ts-CFAR), and a multi-scale fusion of the same statistic is actually the best inshore discriminator---yet we deploy the single channel for its zero parameters, cross-task sharing, and because its margin falls below our pre-registered gate; its value is recall, not per-pixel classification. Injected into a DETR detector's reference points, the prior gives a seed-robust $+1.85$ AR@100 ($+2.1$ small-object recall) at flat mAP, and a random-seed trivial-prior control confirms the \emph{specific} statistic helps where arbitrary high-recall proposals do not (random seeding slightly hurts). Finally, our evaluation-protocol contributions stand independently of the prior's strength: we quantify the substantial chip-level leakage in the de-facto HRSID split (92.7\% any-overlap, 10.8\% by $\ge\!50\%$ area, one full duplicate) and release a leakage-free scene-level re-split, and we measure how the standard Boundary-IoU dilation degenerates to Mask-IoU for the extremely small ships that dominate SAR data (91.5\%\,/\,67\% of HRSID/PSeg instances), motivating a multi-$d$ report.

We deliberately make no aggregate mask-AP state-of-the-art claim; our contribution is a diagnostic framework with a capacity-attributed minimal remedy, a rigorously bounded study of a statistical prior, and a reproducible evaluation protocol. Three directions follow naturally. First, the medium-object mask headroom is the larger remaining inshore deficit and is \emph{not} resolution-limited, calling for a shape- or boundary-aware mechanism rather than further resolution. Second, validating the refinement on the leakage-free HRSID scene split---and addressing the dense-scene mask bleed that our per-instance analysis exposed---would test the remedy under a clean protocol. Third, an adaptive, more strongly parameterized refinement is the path toward cross-dataset and private-data deployment, where the resolution-limited regime the current head targets is only one of several mask-quality bottlenecks.
