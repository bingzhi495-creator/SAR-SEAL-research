\begin{abstract}
Instance segmentation of ships in synthetic-aperture-radar (SAR) imagery is markedly harder in \emph{inshore} scenes than offshore, yet most methods improve the architecture or inject priors without first asking \emph{where} the inshore gap comes from. We take a diagnose-before-fix view. Using an oracle-box analysis on a strong query-based baseline (Mask2Former-R50), we decompose the inshore high-IoU mask gap (AP$_{75}$) into a detection-coverage component ($\sim$60\%) and a mask-resolution component ($\sim$40\%). Guided by this decomposition, we apply a \emph{minimal} remedy---recomputing only the final-layer mask at half resolution ($1/4\!\rightarrow\!1/2$) with a lightweight learned sharpening module---obtaining a seed-robust $+2.6$ AP$_{75}$ on PSeg-SSDD (two seeds each; a larger but single-seed, statistically-suggestive $+4.6$ inshore AP$_{75}$ whose image-level bootstrap CI includes zero), which a bilinear-only control attributes roughly evenly to resolution ($+1.3$) and the learned module ($+1.5$). As a complementary \emph{training-free} component, a coherent-speckle statistical evidence field acts as a shared high-recall prior that mildly but seed-robustly lifts detection recall ($+1.85$ AR, $+2.1$ small-object recall) and seeds a detection front-end, while we honestly characterize where the same prior is \emph{not} useful (per-pixel ship/clutter discrimination). Finally, we contribute two evaluation-protocol findings: a quantification of the substantial chip-level leakage in the de-facto HRSID split (92.7\% of test chips share pixels with training chips; 10.8\% by $\ge$50\% area) with a leakage-free scene-level re-split, and a measurement that the standard Boundary-IoU dilation degenerates to Mask-IoU for the extremely small ships dominating SAR data (91.5\%\,/\,67\% of HRSID/PSeg instances), motivating a multi-$d$ report. We deliberately do not claim state-of-the-art on aggregate mask AP; our contribution is a diagnostic framework with a verified minimal remedy, a rigorous and honest study of a statistical prior, and a reproducible evaluation protocol.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\IEEEPARstart{S}{hips} in SAR imagery are routinely detected and segmented for maritime surveillance, traffic monitoring, and search-and-rescue. While offshore ships---bright targets on a near-homogeneous sea---are now segmented with high accuracy, \emph{inshore} performance lags far behind: on HRSID, inshore mask AP can be roughly half the offshore value, and the per-pixel statistics of ships and man-made shore clutter are not separable in principle. This inshore regime is the practically important one, and closing the gap is the implicit goal of much recent work.

Most of that work proceeds by \emph{adding capacity}: stronger backbones, orientation-aware queries, foundation-model priors, or diffusion decoders. Such additions often improve aggregate numbers, but rarely answer a prior question: for a given strong baseline, what specifically fails inshore---are ships not found, or are found ships poorly masked? Without that decomposition, an architectural gain cannot be attributed to the mechanism it claims, and effort may target a sub-problem that is not the binding constraint.

We argue for a \emph{diagnose-before-fix} methodology and instantiate it for inshore SAR ship segmentation. On a strong, widely-used query-based baseline (Mask2Former-R50), we run an oracle-box analysis measuring, separately, (i) the fraction of inshore ground-truth instances the baseline fails to detect, and (ii) for instances it \emph{does} detect, how far the masks fall short of the high-IoU (AP$_{75}$) criterion. On PSeg-SSDD this decomposes the inshore AP$_{75}$ deficit into $\sim$60\% detection-coverage and $\sim$40\% mask-resolution---the latter a concrete, addressable bottleneck that, to our knowledge, no prior SAR-segmentation method explicitly targets.

Guided by the diagnosis, we apply the \emph{minimal} surgery the analysis prescribes rather than a general capacity increase: we recompute only the final decoder layer's mask at half resolution (from the standard $1/4$ stride to $1/2$) with a small learned sharpening module, leaving the nine auxiliary layers and the matching unchanged. This yields a seed-robust $+2.6$ AP$_{75}$ on PSeg-SSDD (two seeds per configuration; a larger $+4.6$ inshore AP$_{75}$ at seed~0, which a $2000$-resample image-level bootstrap places at a $95\%$ CI of $[-0.5,+9.5]$---suggestive and consistent with the diagnosis, but not significant on the small 46-image inshore stratum, so we headline the overall $+2.6$), and a bilinear-only control attributes the gain roughly evenly to the resolution increase ($+1.3$) and to the learned sharpening ($+1.5$)---so we claim ``higher mask resolution \emph{and} lightweight learned refinement'', not pure resolution. Crucially, the gain materialises exactly where the oracle predicted, giving it explanatory weight beyond a tuned number. We do not observe robust gains in overall AP or small-object AP (the latter within seed noise), and we report this plainly. We further validate the head cross-dataset on HRSID (denser scenes, official split), where it is essentially \emph{neutral} (AP$_{75}$ $+0.5$, within single-seed noise) yet does no harm; crucially, on HRSID's $76$ real large instances the large-object AP is flat ($-0.2$), showing that an apparent $-13.7$ large-object drop on PSeg-SSDD was small-sample noise (PSeg-SSDD has only two large instances) rather than a regression---a conclusion only a cross-dataset check can establish.

Alongside the segmentation main line, we examine whether a \emph{training-free} SAR statistical prior can help---held to the same evidential bar, with honest negatives. We build a multi-scale nonparametric evidence (MNE) field from coherent-speckle statistics. The field is \emph{not} a good per-pixel ship/clutter classifier (it does not beat a box-filter $z$-score by a meaningful margin on either dataset), consistent with the non-separability of ship and shore clutter; but it \emph{is} a useful high-recall signal---evidence seeds injected into a DETR detector's reference points give a seed-robust $+1.85$ AR ($+2.1$ small-object recall) at no mAP cost, with a random-seed control isolating the statistic from any high-recall proposal. This ``useful for recall, not for discrimination'' characterization is, we argue, more valuable than an over-claimed prior.

Finally, while building a faithful evaluation we uncovered two protocol issues with measurable impact. First, the de-facto HRSID chip split leaks heavily: 92.7\% of test chips share at least one pixel with a training chip and 10.8\% overlap by $\ge$50\% of their area, so reported numbers partly measure memorization; we publish a leakage-free scene-level re-split and the official-vs-scene gap. Second, the standard Boundary-IoU dilation (2\% of the image diagonal) degenerates to plain Mask-IoU for the extremely small ships dominating SAR data (91.5\% of HRSID, 67\% of PSeg instances), so a single-$d$ Boundary-IoU is not an independent quality axis here; we report a multi-$d$ curve.

\noindent\textbf{Contributions.} (1) A diagnose-before-fix framework that decomposes the inshore SAR mask gap via oracle-box analysis and prescribes a \emph{minimal} remedy whose gain is verified to land where the diagnosis predicts (seed-robust $+2.6$ AP$_{75}$, with a resolution/learned-refinement decomposition). (2) A rigorous, honest study of a training-free coherent-speckle evidence prior as a shared high-recall signal for both detection and segmentation---including the negative result that it does not discriminate ship from clutter---each evidence use paired with an evidence-removed or trivial-prior control. (3) Two reproducible evaluation-protocol contributions: quantified HRSID-split leakage with a leakage-free scene-level re-split, and the SAR-small-ship Boundary-IoU degeneration with a multi-$d$ report. (4) A dual-delivery evaluation in which detection and segmentation each play to their strengths over a shared prior. We do not claim state-of-the-art on aggregate mask AP; we aim for correctly-attributed, honestly-bounded advances.

\subsection{Query-based segmentation and mask refinement}
Mask2Former and MaskDINO produce masks by a dot product between per-query embeddings and a $1/4$-stride mask feature, capping fine-boundary fidelity for tiny objects. Per-instance or coarse-to-fine refinement is a mature paradigm---RefineMask, PointRend, Mask Transfiner, DynaMask---typically adding $1$--$3$ AP$_{75}$. We do not claim the refinement \emph{paradigm} as novel; our contribution is (a) diagnosing that mask resolution is the binding inshore constraint in SAR via oracle analysis, and (b) the minimal, final-layer-only instantiation suited to single-GPU SAR practice, with an ablation separating resolution from the learned module.

\subsection{Statistical and CFAR priors in SAR}
Constant-false-alarm-rate (CFAR) detection and its truncated/ordered-statistics variants are classical high-recall, low-precision ship locators; the two-stage ``CFAR candidate then learned discriminator'' pattern encodes that such priors localize but do not classify. We position MNE in this lineage and, rather than claiming a novel discriminative prior, \emph{quantify} the high-recall/low-precision behaviour and use it only where sound (recall, seeding), reporting the negative discrimination result explicitly.

\subsection{Data leakage and segmentation evaluation protocols}
Leakage quantification is an established methodological concern in ML; in SAR/remote sensing, chip-tiling of a few large scenes risks cross-split overlap. We are, to our knowledge, the first to quantify the HRSID official-split leakage and release a scene-level re-split. Boundary IoU \cite{cheng2021boundary} notes it approaches Mask IoU for small objects; we measure how severe this is in the SAR small-ship regime and report a multi-$d$ curve to keep an independent boundary axis.

\section{Conclusion}
We presented a diagnose-before-fix study of inshore SAR ship instance segmentation. Rather than adding capacity blindly, we used an oracle-box analysis to decompose the inshore high-IoU mask gap into detection-coverage and mask-resolution components, and applied the minimal remedy the diagnosis prescribed---a final-layer half-resolution mask with lightweight learned sharpening---obtaining a seed-robust $+2.6$ AP$_{75}$ whose source we attribute, via a bilinear control, to resolution and learned refinement in roughly equal parts, and which materializes precisely where the diagnosis predicted. We further gave an honest account of a training-free coherent-speckle evidence prior as a shared high-recall signal---useful for detection recall and seeding, but explicitly not a ship/clutter discriminator---each claim guarded by an evidence-removed or trivial control. Lastly, we contributed two reproducible evaluation-protocol results: quantified HRSID-split leakage with a leakage-free re-split, and the small-ship Boundary-IoU degeneration with a multi-$d$ report. We did not pursue aggregate-AP records; we pursued correctly-attributed, honestly-bounded advances and a reproducible protocol. Future work includes a detect-and-segment cascade operationalizing the shared prior across both tasks, and improving inshore seed precision where the speckle statistic is fundamentally limited.
