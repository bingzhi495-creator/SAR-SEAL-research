% measure it correctly before attributing any fix. (1) We show the de-facto HRSID
% chip split leaks 92.76% of test chips into training (10.75% by >=50% area) and
% release a leakage-free scene-level re-split (3659/1945). (2) We introduce a
% capacity-independent, AP-additive oracle that, holding detection fixed, substitutes
% ground-truth masks and re-runs COCO evaluation to decompose the high-IoU deficit
% into a mask-quality axis and a detection-coverage axis on one additive scale. The
% decomposition reveals a REGIME-DEPENDENT TWO-WAY constraint, NOT a single universal
% bottleneck: on PSeg-SSDD the inshore split is co-equal (51% mask / 49% coverage,
% the 95% CI straddling 50%), shifting to 69/31 overall and 87/13 offshore, i.e. the
% mask share grows with base accuracy; on the denser HRSID it inverts to 43% mask /
% 57% COVERAGE-dominated. (3) This measurement PREDICTS the behaviour of a fix: a
% capacity-controlled learned mask refinement recovers part of the mask axis on
% PSeg-SSDD (+3.0 AP75, 3-seed; an equal-parameter control attributes the gain to
% capacity, not resolution), and -- as the oracle predicts from HRSID's
% coverage-dominance -- does NOT transfer to HRSID. (4) We show Boundary-IoU collapses
% to Mask-IoU for >91% of SAR ship instances. We make no aggregate-AP state-of-the-art
% claim; the contribution is the instruments to measure the high-IoU deficit correctly
% and an honestly-attributed account of where each axis binds.
