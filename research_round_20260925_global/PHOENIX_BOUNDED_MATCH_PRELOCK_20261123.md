# Exact bounded matching between fixed pairs and independent native endpoints

Date: 2026-11-23. Freeze this comparison before running any of its mask or label calculations on study data. This is a mechanism contrast on two historical cohorts with frozen Phoenix fields, not a new acquisition test. [Previous comparison and verified metrics](PHOENIX_PAIR_OPERATOR_ACTUAL_20261123.md).

## Contrast and feasible actions

Retain the frozen RF-DETR 5202 detections and original 74/42 Instance-RSDD views, and the frozen D-FINE detections and original 232/222 PSeg-SSDD views. Preserve the previous native masks, candidates, score ordering, endpoint lists, exact binary32 Phoenix values, signed counts and partitions. Every positive-quota candidate must write exactly its historical k background additions and k foreground removals. All other predictions stay native. Keep 29,900 additions and 29,900 removals per arm on Instance-RSDD and 73,770 of each sign on PSeg-SSDD. Use the existing full-candidate COCO scorer and continuous polygon matching. No model training, new prediction, hyperparameter search or geographic data.

F is the previously accepted fixed pairing mask, I the previously accepted independent mask. For each candidate, calculate its squared-distance cap r² from the largest squared displacement among the k pairs selected by F. Admit all add-to-drop edges on the same original support whose squared Euclidean distance is at most r². L chooses exactly k disjoint edges maximizing the exact sum of addition minus removal probabilities. On a probability-objective tie choose the lexicographically earliest selected set in historical probability order (addition side first; descending additions or ascending removals; input order breaks equal endpoint scores). For that set choose minimum total squared edge distance, then deterministic solver order. If I can be paired under the cap it already has the lexicographically first optimal endpoint sets and is used with minimum feasible distance. F itself is feasible, and I attains the unconstrained maximum. Verify J(F) ≤ J(L) ≤ J(I) exactly, each endpoint used at most once, the cap for each chosen edge, and mask quotas and area before the target labels open.

Publish the number of I candidates that can be matched under the cap, counts of different L and I masks, pair distance and realized quota aggregates, and prior two-arm mask/metric parity. A radius inherited from F controls its largest move per candidate, not its mean displacement or topology. Distance-bounded transport and cardinality assignment are established optimization constructs. This study tests whether that simple stronger comparator explains the gains in the previous SAR experiment. [Cardinality assignment](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0166-218X(00)00301-2) · [Pixel swapping](https://doi.org/10.14358/PERS.71.7.839)

## Main decision and sensitivity

Report I−L as the primary contrast, L−F as the explanatory contrast, on both complete views and their nested sensitivity views. Within a view report pooled mask AP, AP75, AP90 and AR100, original .50 match retention and descriptively rematched coverage, plus fixed-match continuous IoU and truth-normalized FP/FN. Reuse the original prefix rules, area restriction, geometry weighting and 4,096 paired PCG64(5217) resamples. A directional advancement in either contrast needs absolute AP gain of at least 0.30 points, a strictly positive lower 95% continuous IoU bound in the same direction, and at least 20 differing fixed eligible hulls across 8 groups. Apply the symmetric rule for a loss. AP is a point estimate in this rule.

If L−F clears this joint threshold and abs(AP(I−L)) is at most 0.15 with the entire continuous-IoU interval inside [−0.15, 0.15], call L a practical recovery under these declared tolerances for that view. This is a geometry interval and AP point tolerance, not a formal equivalence test of AP. If I−L clears the joint threshold, the inherited displacement restriction leaves measurable benefit on that view. Otherwise keep the observed effects, interval and exposure without choosing a winner by AP alone. Apply the rules separately on two historical detector/dataset combinations and never count the nested sensitivities as independent. Publish both sides even if mixed.
