For grayscale, use the original float32 luminance transform [0.299, 0.587, 0.114] divided by 255 on the decoded original RGB. Within x's chip let r(x) = (number of pixels strictly darker than x + half the number equal to x) / total pixel count. An appearance-only baseline uses r(x) without mask neighbors. For the aligned vote, sample the original static-attraction disk of radius five native pixels with the center excluded. Use w(d) = exp(-d/5), affinity a(x,y) = 1 - abs(r(x)-r(y)) and a weighted average of the binary native mask M with normalized weights w(d) a(x,y). For image exterior, use a neutral affinity of 1 and a zero native mask. Current in-image midranks and the exterior convention ensure a positive denominator.

The radial control replaces in-image affinities within each exactly equal integer squared-distance shell around x with that shell's in-image mean. Exterior entries keep neutral affinity. Preserve the same denominator in both arms. Aligned minus radial vote is the weighted sum of within-shell affinity–foreground covariance divided by the shared denominator. The radial score is the expected score under reassignment of affinity among the in-image positions within shells. Nonlinear top-k selection can differ from the expected score's selection. This comparison controls the radial affinity profile, not the complete image marginal distribution at other endpoints. Uniform affinity recovers the original normalized static scores. Use the original endpoint ordering ties (prior 1e-12 attraction tolerance at original kernel scale) under the same deterministic ranker after normalizing.

These are native-pixel variations of established spatial attraction and image-guided bilateral filtering. PAMR uses neighboring appearance to propagate masks. PAR uses RGB and spatial affinities for refinement. Here the mechanism contrast isolates the within-shell correspondence of image affinity and mask support under an actual signed budget.
