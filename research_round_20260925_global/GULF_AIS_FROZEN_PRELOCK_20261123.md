# Frozen AIS length study on SAR Ship Gulf

## Scope and untouched sample

Test whether freeing a boundary-point pair under identical signed edit counts reduces the absolute error of a declared vessel-length proxy on a new set of Sentinel-1 A open-water acquisitions. The experiment uses the original Gulf release and nominal native 10 m samples. It measures a projected mask extent against AIS length. The author release supplies no manual hull polygons or beam measurements, and the detector-to-AIS association remains a proxy. This evaluation supplies a distinct physical test alongside the manuscript's mask benchmarks. ## Length response and decision

Use the native AIS crop center as square-array index (x, y) = (256, 256). Traverse unchanged candidate ranks until finding the first candidate with a foreground pixel center at distance at most 3 original pixels from this point. Use 8-connectivity. Among that mask's components meeting the same radius choose the minimum nearest-cell squared distance, then larger area, then lowest row-major component start. The same native candidate, component and axis are shared by every strategy. For every edited mask choose the component with the largest positive overlap with the fixed native component, with larger area then lowest row-major start as deterministic ties. A missing positive overlap, a selected component touching the chip edge or fewer than two centers gives that arm a measurement failure. Retain original and common paired coverage, with counts by arm. Do not use AIS declared dimensions to select any identity or component.

For each common paired anchor fix the unit major axis of the raw component from the covariance of its native pixel centers. For isotropic covariance use the column axis. Project each arm's selected full pixel cells onto that axis and multiply the span by 10 m. The full-cell span in pixels is the maximum minus minimum dot product of centers plus `abs(axis_x) + abs(axis_y)`. This is an apparent native-axis length proxy. Ground-range and azimuth sampling are finer than the approximately 20–23 m spatial resolution stated for the SAR product. The raw raster axes do not encode an aligned geographic heading. The AIS heading and geographic frame are not used. Open the declared length only after the common anchor, axis and four spans for that chip are fixed. Admissible length is finite, at least the author's 50 m source minimum. All four arms use the same AIS scalar. As a diagnostic retain the original whole-mask lengths along the same axis for those common anchors, when their masks do not touch the chip edge.

Main effect is the equal-orbit/date-group mean of `abs(length_fixed − AIS) − abs(length_independent_at_fixed_count − AIS)` in metres. Keep common anchors with zero native fixed-pair budget as zero. Use 4,096 paired group bootstrap draws (PCG64 seed 5217) and 95% percentile bounds, with UTC date-only sensitivity when feasible. Advance this physical claim only with at least 20 common measurable positive-fixed-budget ships across at least eight selected orbit/date groups, point estimate at least +0.5 m and positive lower bound. Report absolute errors, signed biases, failures, coverage, common measurable counts, component versus whole extent, and dependency/runtime.
