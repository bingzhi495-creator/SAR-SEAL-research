## Spatial controls on the same components

Use the VV annotation geolocation grid at zero-based native sample centers as defined by ESA product specification table 6-91. Source index is crop-center index plus chip index minus 256. Sample centers use no half-cell shift. Project the four corners of each selected full native pixel cell at offsets ±0.5. No extrapolation or out-of-grid point is admitted. Use bilinear interpolation inside rectangular neighboring grid knots in the original grid, and WGS84 inverse geodesics to express positions as local east and north about each fixed crop center. These estimates are approximate local geocoding, with unknown moving-target displacement. ## Timing control and inference boundary

Compare the annotated sample-center azimuthTime interpolated at each native crop center with the time midpoint used by the source exporter. The archive stores the absolute reported AIS time lag d. With h = absolute difference between the local sample timestamp and the scene midpoint, the possible absolute timestamp offsets are abs(d−h) and d+h. Multiplying by the original reported SOG gives a constant-speed distance proxy, with nautical knots converted by 1852/3600. Summarize both endpoints and count crossings of the original 50 m speed×lag proxy threshold. Leave association and inclusion fixed and keep every arm's masks. The zero-Doppler timestamp and AIS report time have different observation semantics, the sign of the original lag is unavailable, and author missing-as-zero speed cannot distinguish all physical conditions. These numbers do not resolve moving-target Doppler displacement or vessel identity.

Report unweighted per-ship summaries and group-equal paired differences as appropriate, with 4,096 paired orbit/date bootstrap draws at PCG64 seed 5217 and 95% percentile bounds for the fixed-arm mean-error comparisons. Label all posthoc diagnostics and preserve the prior physical primary outcome. No physical calibration, selection changes, or deployment threshold are inferred from the known cohort.
