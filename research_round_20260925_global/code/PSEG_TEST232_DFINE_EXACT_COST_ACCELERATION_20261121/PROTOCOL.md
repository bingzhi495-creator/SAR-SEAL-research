# Exact CPU probability sampling comparison

The original frozen D-FINE natural refinement profile uses 32 evenly spaced image ordinals in the 232-image official order. Native mask support and probability sampling account for the largest shares. The existing support routine already crops its morphology to the mask extent. Test one localized replacement of the probability sampler.

The original sampler converts the entire 160 × 160 probability plane to binary16 and back to binary32 once per queried side, then gathers the four neighboring source pixels. The replacement gathers those four source values first, rounds each the same way, and reuses native half-pixel coordinates tabulated once per image. Bilinear products and additions occur in the same order, and output is converted to binary16 as before. Keep the original native point support, feature construction, head, thresholds, Hungarian matching, tie rules, selection and RLE encoding. The unchanged native shape adapter is constructed by both arms. Only the replacement adds coordinate tabulation to its timed invocation.

For each of all 232 images, first run one original and one optimized warm invocation. In the optimized warm invocation, compare every requested sample against the original sampler as unsigned 16-bit patterns. Then time two pairs of complete natural refinements without instrumentation, with reversed arm order on the second pair and alternating first arm across images. Both arms receive identical resident RGB, probability planes, native RLE and candidate records. Verify full ordered candidate records including exact compressed RLE after every invocation. A mismatch fails the experiment. Report per-image average of the two measurements, paired mean, median, p95, both full-population repetition totals, ratio of aggregate original to optimized, and the numbers of images faster, unchanged or slower. No target labels, subsampling, data-dependent stopping, detector calls or parameter tuning.

Record input preparation separately. The measurements are single-thread CPU add-on timings on a Windows host with a concurrent HRSID CPU workload. They do not measure integrated device transfer, detector FPS or GPU speed.
