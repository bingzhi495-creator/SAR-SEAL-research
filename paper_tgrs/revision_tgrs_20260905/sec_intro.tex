\begin{abstract}
Synthetic aperture radar (SAR) ship instance segmentation remains difficult in crowded harbors, where overlapping detections, coarse contours, and unreliable confidence ordering can persist after model training. This article studies a postprocessing cascade for Mask2Former-R50 carriers initialized from Common Objects in Context (COCO) weights and frozen after fitting. The HRSID carrier uses auxiliary mask-quality supervision; the PSeg-SSDD carrier uses the standard head. Adopted weight averaging and multiscale flip test-time augmentation supply aligned candidates. A cluster-support-gated fusion operator groups them by mask intersection-over-union and applies pixel voting only within a bounded cluster-size range; other groups retain their highest-scoring seed. Support denotes candidate cardinality, not the number of distinct views. A separately trained boundary-patch refiner updates only a morphological boundary band, and a learned quality regressor adjusts scores without changing masks. Both correctors use predictions and existing labels from the training split. In the historical HRSID protocol, the selected cascade changes official mask average precision from 45.6 to 59.4 and inshore mask average precision from 27.7 to 44.5. On PSeg-SSDD, a newly trained carrier and dataset-specific correctors yield a change from 60.9 to 67.5 official mask average precision while reusing selected processing settings. An exact replay of the HRSID eight-view candidates finds that per-view top-1 voting exceeds the gated fusion by 2.6 official mask AP points without changing candidates or scores; after applying the stored boundary refiner, the difference is 0.25 points in favor of top-1. These observations are exploratory: HRSID test subsets informed configuration selection, the stronger simple fusion has not been carried through a refitted full cascade, and neither study establishes an acquisition-disjoint advantage.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\IEEEPARstart{S}{ynthetic} aperture radar (SAR) supports maritime observation under cloud cover and at night. Instance segmentation adds vessel contours to detection, providing spatial information for applications such as harbor monitoring and vessel-geometry analysis. Pixel-annotated datasets, including HRSID and PSeg-SSDD, enable evaluation of this task across offshore and inshore scenes \cite{hrsid2020,ssdd2021}. In crowded harbors, bright shore structures, adjacent ships, and weak hull contrast make instance separation and accurate delineation particularly challenging. These conditions also expose an inference problem: several plausible predictions may describe the same vessel, yet their outlines and confidence scores need not agree.

Recent SAR segmentation methods address these difficulties through specialized representations and decoders. Examples include low-level feature guidance \cite{lfgnet2022}, orientation-aware queries \cite{o2former2026}, diffusion-based instance decoding \cite{diffsar2025}, Gaussian-heatmap instance encoding \cite{gaussheatmap2024}, and adaptation of foundation segmentation models \cite{sarsam2025}. These directions concern how a model learns and represents targets. Once a model has been fitted, a complementary question remains: how should its overlapping predictions be associated, converted into one mask per instance, and ranked? This question is relevant when multiple transformed inputs are evaluated with an existing segmenter and retraining the main network is not part of the inference procedure.

Three aspects of the output motivate the design studied here. First, the candidate set must suppress duplicates while retaining neighboring vessels. Bounding boxes can overlap substantially even when elongated ship masks occupy different pixels, making mask intersection-over-union (IoU) a useful association criterion. Second, combining candidates can change a contour: averaging or voting may reconcile a disagreement, but it can also replace an adequate seed boundary with a poorer one. Third, a retained mask may still have local boundary errors or a confidence score that does not reflect its spatial quality. These are distinct intervention points, although their effects on average precision (AP) remain coupled through matching and ranking.

We address them with a postprocessing cascade around a fixed, already trained carrier (Fig.~\ref{fig:framework}). The HRSID carrier is a Mask2Former with a ResNet-50 backbone \cite{mask2former2022}, initialized from Common Objects in Context (COCO) instance segmentation weights and fitted with auxiliary mask-quality supervision. The PSeg-SSDD carrier instead uses the standard Mask2Former head. Each is frozen after training, and these training identities are part of the respective protocols. Weight averaging following model soups \cite{modelsoups2022} and multiscale horizontal-flip test-time augmentation (TTA) are adopted to generate candidates. Our fusion operator processes candidates in score order, groups unused masks by their IoU with a seed, and uses the number of candidates in a cluster to select its output rule. A bounded support range activates pixel voting, with foreground retained at ties; clusters outside that range keep their seed. The support variable counts candidates and can include multiple predictions from one view. The gate is therefore a cluster-cardinality heuristic, rather than a measure of independent cross-view agreement or a guarantee of mask quality.

Figure~\ref{fig:framework} summarizes the pipeline. Two supervised correctors then operate on the fused instances. A boundary-patch refiner adopts the local re-segmentation principle of BPR \cite{bpr2021}. It reads the SAR patch and coarse mask, and writes its prediction only inside a prescribed morphological band around the coarse boundary. A separate mask-quality regressor uses candidate-support, score, and geometry features to recalibrate the output scores. Both are fitted using predictions and existing annotations from the training split; neither updates the fitted carrier during postprocessing. The refiner preserves pixels outside its allowed band and leaves scores unchanged, whereas the regressor leaves masks unchanged. These interfaces make changes traceable, but do not by themselves imply additive gains or interchangeable execution order.

The experiments evaluate these choices through fusion controls on the same candidate pool, a progressive cascade, and repetitions with additional datasets and carriers. On HRSID, the reported official mask AP changes from 45.6 for the base carrier to 59.4 for the complete cascade, and inshore mask AP from 27.7 to 44.5. These are exploratory results: HRSID test subsets were used to select inference configurations. A later exact eight-view replay shows that a simpler per-view top-1 vote reaches 56.4 official AP before either corrector, compared with 53.8 for the selected gate. Applying the historically selected BPR weights to both outputs gives 58.81 versus 58.56 official AP; a refitted, out-of-fold top-1 cascade including re-ranking has not been assessed. On PSeg-SSDD, selected processing settings are reused while a standard-head carrier is newly trained and both correctors are fitted to that dataset; the reported official mask AP changes from 60.9 to 67.5. This tests the recipe under dataset-specific fitting, not frozen-model cross-dataset transfer or independent confirmation of every HRSID design conclusion. Literature-reported results are included as context; differences in initialization, model selection, and computational budgets preclude treating them as a single matched ranking.

The contributions are as follows.
\begin{itemize}
\item A documented comparison of mask-IoU association and candidate-cardinality-gated voting on augmented SAR predictions. The matched per-view top-1 control outperforms the selected gate on all three HRSID test partitions, so these observations do not support the gate as a necessary algorithmic contribution.
\item A defined interface between fusion, an adopted boundary-patch refiner, and learned score calibration. Training-split predictions provide the correction inputs, existing labels provide supervision, and explicit write constraints delimit which parts of each output can change. The contribution is the specified integration and its evaluation, rather than the invention of mask voting or boundary-patch refinement.
\item An empirical characterization of the cascade on HRSID and PSeg-SSDD, together with component and carrier controls. The study separates exploratory configuration selection, transferred settings, and literature context, and reports the observed scope of the gains without attributing them to a general preservation principle or an isolated pretraining effect.
\end{itemize}
