\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{SAR Ship Instance Segmentation}
\label{sec:related-sar}
SAR ship instance segmentation combines recognition with spatial delineation under scattering-dependent appearance and coastal clutter. HRSID \cite{hrsid2020} and the pixel-level SSDD annotations \cite{ssdd2021} support evaluation beyond bounding-box detection. General instance segmenters, including Mask R-CNN \cite{maskrcnn2017} and Mask2Former \cite{mask2former2022}, provide reusable foundations. SAR-specific approaches add mechanisms aimed at the imaging conditions or target geometry: LFG-Net uses low-level feature guidance and super-resolution denoising \cite{lfgnet2022}; O2Former introduces query generation and orientation-aware modeling \cite{o2former2026}; DiffSARShipInst uses diffusion-based instance decoding and asymmetric-convolution refinement \cite{diffsar2025}; and Gaussian-heatmap encoding offers an alternative to bounding-box-based instance representation \cite{gaussheatmap2024}. SARSAM studies parameter-efficient adaptation of a foundation segmentation model \cite{sarsam2025}.

Architectural design and training protocol both affect such comparisons. Work on SARDet-100K also studies pretraining transfer for SAR detection \cite{sardet100k2024}, emphasizing the relevance of the initialization regime. Our HRSID experiments use a COCO-initialized Mask2Former-R50 carrier trained with an auxiliary mask-quality objective, whereas the PSeg-SSDD carrier uses the standard head. Comparisons with results obtained using different initializations or schedules cannot isolate either pretraining or architecture as the cause of an AP difference. The focus here is the behavior of candidate aggregation and subsequent correction after this carrier has been fitted. It complements representation-level methods and does not establish that a stronger representation, different loss, or longer training schedule is generally unnecessary.

\subsection{Candidate Aggregation and Mask Voting}
\label{sec:related-tta}
Weight averaging and TTA provide established ways to obtain or combine predictions. Model soups average parameters of models fine-tuned from a common initialization \cite{modelsoups2022}. TTA instead evaluates transformed inputs, requiring prediction alignment and an aggregation rule. In box space, Soft-NMS reduces scores according to overlap \cite{softnms2017}, whereas weighted boxes fusion combines box coordinates \cite{wbf2021}. Learned weighting of augmented predictions has also been studied for classification \cite{shanmugam2021}. These methods motivate careful treatment of aggregation as part of inference; they do not make an arbitrary merge rule appropriate for every candidate representation.

Mask voting has direct precedents in instance segmentation. MNC aggregates related mask predictions by classification-score-weighted pixel averaging after box-based suppression, then binarizes the result \cite{mnc2016}. FCIS also forms final masks by weighted pixel-level aggregation of overlapping instance predictions \cite{fcis2017}. Consequently, combining mask hypotheses is established practice. Relative to these predecessors, the present rule uses binary mask IoU for score-seeded association of aligned TTA candidates and conditions unweighted, foreground-tie-preserving voting on a finite cluster-size band. A singleton or a cluster above the band retains its seed. The proposed object of study is this particular association-and-selection rule in the evaluated SAR setting. Soup, TTA, pixel voting, and the broader idea of inference aggregation are adopted ingredients. Because cluster membership is not restricted to one candidate per view, the cardinality gate is also distinct from a gate defined on the number of agreeing views.

\subsection{Boundary Correction and Mask-Quality Calibration}
\label{sec:related-peft}
\label{sec:related-aniso}
Boundary refinement addresses errors that may remain after an instance has been detected. PointRend predicts labels at selected points through a learned segmentation head \cite{pointrend2020}; SegFix estimates boundary-to-interior offsets for correcting segmentation outputs \cite{segfix2020}. BPR extracts patches along predicted instance boundaries and applies a separate high-resolution refinement network to the output of an existing segmenter \cite{bpr2021}. Our refiner adopts this BPR principle and the use of a coarse prediction as an input. Its specification for this study consists of SAR-intensity and coarse-mask channels, training-split prediction/label pairs, and a morphological restriction on where the patch predictions may be written back. Local re-segmentation and post-hoc boundary correction are therefore not claimed as new concepts. The band defines a preservation constraint on output pixels; whether it contributes to accuracy is an empirical question.

Spatial accuracy and confidence calibration are related but different objectives. IoU-Net estimates localization quality for boxes \cite{iounet2018}, and Mask Scoring R-CNN regresses mask IoU to improve alignment between mask quality and detection score \cite{msrcnn2019}. The auxiliary training objective of our HRSID carrier belongs to this family of mask-quality supervision. The subsequent external re-ranker is a separate component: it estimates quality from saved support statistics, detection scores, and mask geometry, then blends that estimate with the existing score. It changes ordering without changing mask pixels. The refiner and re-ranker share a supervised correction workflow based on training annotations, while targeting different output variables. Their combined effect must be evaluated because changing a mask can alter its overlap with ground truth and changing scores can alter matching order. The interface provides traceable edits, rather than a proof that the two operations commute or improve every AP endpoint.
