\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{What the Decomposition Establishes}\label{sec:disc-preservation}
The most direct evidence in this study is the comparison of merge rules on a fixed candidate pool, followed by changes within the same trained carrier. Mask-space seed clustering accounts for part of the improvement over box-NMS; conditional voting adds a smaller inshore increment while retaining the seed-only offshore result among the originally tested rules. The matched per-view top-1 vote subsequently exceeds the gate on inshore, full, and offshore HRSID test partitions. Thus, the cluster-cardinality gate is not supported as a necessary algorithmic component by the current candidate-pool evidence. The stored BPR narrows the official top-1 advantage to 0.25 AP points and reverses the offshore ordering; this is an observational interaction on exposed test data, not a mechanism estimate. Re-ranking after top-1 has not been measured.

The write constraints make each component auditable, but do not imply commuting operators or guaranteed AP gains. Fusion changes the candidate set and its geometry; BPR changes mask IoU, and consequently the quality that a score should predict. Re-ranking changes the score order used by matching and top-ranked evaluation. Their interaction must therefore be assessed in the chosen sequence. The unchanged-pixel guarantee of BPR and unchanged-mask guarantee of re-ranking are implementation properties, distinct from claims about accuracy.

The architecture probes motivate hypotheses rather than a preservation law. For example, O2Former reports a 25.2-point inshore improvement over its 17.9 baseline under its own protocol \cite{o2former2025}. Earlier local Gaussian--Wasserstein matching experiments report 12.5 to 16.9 (+4.4) with a from-scratch head, comparing a single-seed base with a two-seed result, and 27.9 to 27.4 in a single-seed COCO-initialized comparison. These observations involve different training and aggregation conditions. Together with the 0.9-point SGA inshore loss, they suggest that training regime and operator design matter, but do not isolate pretraining, representation preservation, or capacity as causal explanations. In particular, the HRSID carrier itself received auxiliary mask-quality supervision, whereas the PSeg-SSDD carrier uses the standard head.

\subsection{Limits and Confirmatory Evaluation}\label{sec:disc-limitations}
The principal limitation is selection. HRSID fusion settings were chosen on test results, the reported BPR instance is the best of three examined training seeds, and several comparison checkpoints were selected on test data. The bootstrap intervals condition on those decisions. The matched top-1 and reused-BPR results are also on the historically exposed HRSID test set and cannot be recycled as independent model-selection or generalization estimates. They remove two missing-control gaps while overturning the gate's claimed necessity; the historical complete cascade has not been compared with a top-1 cascade whose correctors are trained out of fold. A confirmatory study should allocate development images by parent SAR scene where possible, select the carrier, soup membership, readout, and correctors without access to the final evaluation set, and report pre-specified seeds and scene-level uncertainty. Repeating the recipe on PSeg-SSDD with a new carrier and refitted correctors provides exploratory evidence of portability under dataset-specific fitting, but its 46-image inshore subset yields intervals crossing zero for two stage increments.

Corrector supervision is another limitation. BPR's random patch holdout can share an image with its fitting patches; the re-ranker's image holdout consists of images already used to fit the carrier. Out-of-fold carrier predictions and image- or scene-disjoint corrector validation are needed to measure the effect of residual-distribution shift. Filename separation and annotation-free application do not settle parent-scene independence. Soup compatibility also needs a rule selected on development data: the six PSeg-SSDD seeds form two empirically distinct averaging groups, and increasing the member count is not consistently beneficial.

The current cost accounting includes three carrier trainings and separate corrector fits. View count alone does not determine latency, because input sizes, candidate counts, patch counts, and device transfers vary. A deployment comparison should measure synchronized mean and tail latency, memory, and per-stage work at fixed hardware, precision, and batching, alongside detection and mask quality. The limited amplitude-image datasets also leave cross-sensor and acquisition-domain transfer unresolved. A radiometric-consistency re-ranking probe returned +0.0 inshore over its flip-TTA control; that negative probe does not establish an information-theoretic sensor ceiling. The final HRSID offshore AP remains 70.1 versus the literature-reported 71.7, a 1.6-point difference, and the PSeg-SSDD differences remain 1.1 inshore and 2.1 offshore. These cross-protocol values provide context, not an estimate of an irreducible error floor.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
This study evaluated mask fusion, boundary-patch refinement, and quality-based score adjustment on trained SAR segmentation carriers. The selected gate uses candidate cardinality to choose between pixel voting and seed retention; the subsequent correctors constrain their writes to a morphological boundary band and detection scores, respectively. The selected historical cascade reports 44.5 inshore mask AP and 48.3 AP$_{75}$ on HRSID, and the separately fitted PSeg-SSDD pipeline reports 62.1 inshore AP. The exact same-pool top-1 control exceeds the gate by 2.6 official mask AP points before BPR and by 0.25 points after the historically selected BPR, so the present evidence does not justify the gate as a necessary new method. Test-informed HRSID selection, in-sample corrector residuals, and parent-scene overlap prevent independent confirmation of the full cascade. PSeg-SSDD uses dataset-specific carrier and corrector fitting and does not establish frozen-model transfer or improvement from every stage. The next step is a newly locked source-disjoint study with strong simple readouts, out-of-fold residual supervision, and measured compute budgets.
