\begin{abstract}
Deep learning has advanced ship instance segmentation in synthetic aperture radar (SAR) imagery. However, the residual error concentrates in the near-shore regime, where small, low-contrast hulls are embedded in bright dock clutter and specialized architectures lose 25 to 30 mask average precision (AP) points relative to their offshore performance. Existing methods respond with further architectural specialization, while the pretraining regime and the fusion of overlapping candidates go unexamined. This article proposes a consensus-gated mask intersection-over-union (IoU) vote fusion operator, which clusters candidates pooled from augmented views by mask IoU and, gated by cross-view agreement, emits either the seed mask or a consensus-denoised mask per cluster; the weight soup and wide multi-scale flip test-time augmentation (TTA) supplying the views are established practice and act only as its carrier. Two self-correcting stages follow: a boundary-patch refiner confined to a four-pixel band edits mask pixels only, a learned consensus re-ranker edits detection scores only, both fitted on the frozen model's own training-split errors without new annotation. A companion study isolates the pretraining regime. Under our 55-epoch protocol, Common Objects in Context (COCO) initialization lifts an unmodified Mask2Former to 27.7 inshore AP, against the 17.9 reported under the 500-epoch from-scratch-head protocol. Seven architecture- and training-side modifications were flat, negative, or absorbed. The complete cascade reaches 44.5/59.4/70.1 mask AP on the HRSID inshore/official/offshore splits with an inshore AP75 of 48.3 and 62.1/67.5/69.8 on PSeg-SSDD. The training-free stack and the refiner lift six carrier--dataset pairs across five distinct architectures on every split.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\IEEEPARstart{W}{ith} its all-weather, day-and-night imaging capability, synthetic aperture radar (SAR) plays a key role in maritime surveillance, fisheries monitoring, and vessel traffic management. Ship instance segmentation extends detection from bounding boxes to pixel-accurate masks and thereby supplies the hull geometry that heading estimation, berthing analysis, and fine-grained recognition require. The release of pixel-annotated benchmarks such as HRSID \cite{hrsid2020} has made the task quantitatively comparable across methods, and deep segmentation networks now dominate the reported results.

Progress, however, splits between two regimes. Offshore, well-resolved ships on open water are handled adequately by generic instance segmentation models; inshore, small hulls with low or even negative contrast are buried in bright, structured dock clutter under single-look speckle, and the specialized architectures published to date lose 25 to 30 mask average precision (AP) points relative to their own offshore performance, while classic detectors and from-scratch baselines lose more than 30. The open problem therefore lives almost entirely in the near-shore split. The prevailing response has been architectural: an optimized query generator with an orientation-aware embedding module \cite{o2former2025}, a diffusion-based instance decoder with asymmetric-convolution mask refinement \cite{diffsar2025}, a bounding-box-free elliptical Gaussian-heatmap encoding \cite{gaussheatmap2024}, low-level feature guidance through super-resolution denoising \cite{lfgnet2022}, and parameter-efficient transfer of the Segment Anything Model \cite{sarsam2025}. These works share one protocol, namely, 500-epoch training with an ImageNet backbone and a from-scratch segmentation head, and all attribute their gains to new operators. Two degrees of freedom remain unexamined on that route. Outside instance segmentation, SARDet-100K studies pretraining transfer for box-level SAR detection \cite{sardet100k2024}. Within SAR ship instance segmentation, no published method isolates the pretraining regime as a variable. Nor does any study the fusion step in which overlapping candidates are merged, although that step decides whether the evidence gathered from multiple views of a scene accumulates or cancels.

This article starts from a measurement instead. Under the from-scratch-head protocol, plain Mask2Former \cite{mask2former2022} scores 17.9 inshore mask AP on HRSID \cite{o2former2025}; the identical architecture initialized from Common Objects in Context (COCO) instance segmentation weights reaches 27.7 after 55 epochs under our protocol. A controlled seven-direction sweep on the stronger base finds no architecture- or training-side modification that pays. We therefore leave the model untouched and recover accuracy behind it with a three-rung inference cascade (Fig.~\ref{fig:framework}). The first rung is training-free and is built around a consensus-gated mask intersection-over-union (IoU) vote fusion operator. It clusters the candidates pooled from augmented views by true mask IoU instead of box IoU and, gated by cross-view agreement, emits either the seed mask or a consensus-denoised mask per cluster. The weight soup \cite{modelsoups2022} and the wide multi-scale flip test-time augmentation (TTA) that supply those candidates are established practice and serve only as its carrier. The remaining two rungs are self-correctors that never retrain or modify the main model. A boundary-patch refiner in the boundary-patch refinement (BPR) family \cite{bpr2021} edits mask pixels only, inside a $\pm 4$-pixel band around predicted boundaries, and a learned consensus re-ranker edits detection scores only. Both are fitted on the frozen base model's own training-split errors, and no new annotation is drawn. On HRSID the cascade carries the 27.7-AP base to 44.5 inshore mask AP and 48.3 inshore AP$_{75}$, above the strongest reported 500-epoch specialized architecture (O2Former, 43.1 and 45.9) at 55 epochs of training and roughly $8\times$ inference cost, although that architecture keeps a 1.6-point offshore lead. On PSeg-SSDD the same recipe reaches 62.1 inshore mask AP, above all six comparison methods we trained under one protocol. Applied without per-carrier tuning to six further carrier--dataset pairs across five distinct architectures, the training-free stack and the refiner improve every pair on every split.

The main contributions of this article are summarized as follows.
\begin{itemize}
\item A consensus-gated mask-IoU vote fusion operator that clusters cross-view candidates by mask IoU and, within a cross-view agreement band, chooses between the seed mask and a consensus-denoised mask per cluster. The weight soup and the wide TTA supplying the views serve only as its carrier. On identical candidates the operator adds +1.7/+1.2/+0.6 inshore/official/offshore AP over box non-maximum suppression (NMS), and its value is that it makes wide TTA safe. Widening the view set from six to eight under box-NMS lowers offshore AP from 64.4 to 64.2, whereas the consensus vote restores 64.8. The fusion operator, not the view count, therefore decides whether aggressive TTA helps or harms.
\item A self-correcting inference cascade of two small correctors that write disjoint fields: a boundary-patch refiner confined to a $\pm 4$-pixel band around predicted boundaries, which edits mask pixels only, and a 14-feature gradient-boosted consensus re-ranker, which edits detection scores only. Both are fitted on the frozen base model's own errors on the training split, draw no new annotation, and are audited against ground-truth leakage.
\item A pretraining-regime study with full-recipe results. Under our 55-epoch protocol, COCO initialization moves the unmodified base to 27.7 inshore AP, 9.8 above the reported from-scratch-head result, and a controlled seven-direction sweep finds every architecture- and training-side modification flat, negative, or absorbed. The complete recipe reaches 44.5 inshore AP and 48.3 inshore AP$_{75}$ on HRSID, and its training-free stack and refiner lift six further carrier--dataset pairs across five distinct architectures.
\end{itemize}
