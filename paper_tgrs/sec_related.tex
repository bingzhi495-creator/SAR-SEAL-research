\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{SAR Ship Instance Segmentation}
\label{sec:related-sar}
Published SAR ship instance segmentation methods are predominantly specialized architectures trained from scratch, that is, an ImageNet-pretrained backbone with randomly initialized detection and mask heads, under long schedules on HRSID \cite{hrsid2020} and PSeg-SSDD \cite{ssdd2021}. LFG-Net exploits low-level feature guidance with super-resolution denoising to counter the scattering and noise characteristics of near-shore scenes \cite{lfgnet2022}; a bounding-box-free Gaussian-heatmap formulation with centroid- and orientation-constrained losses reports 42.5 inshore mask AP on HRSID \cite{gaussheatmap2024}; and DiffSARShipInst couples a diffusion-based instance decoder with aspect-ratio-matched asymmetric-convolution mask refinement and reports 42.6 inshore \cite{diffsar2025}. O2Former holds the strongest split-matched inshore result to date, 43.1 mask AP, by augmenting Mask2Former \cite{mask2former2022} with an optimized query generator and an orientation-aware polar-coordinate module under a 500-epoch from-scratch protocol in which its own Mask2Former baseline reaches only 17.9 inshore \cite{o2former2025}. SARSAM adapts the Segment Anything Model by parameter-efficient tuning but reports only AP at IoU 0.50, which precludes full-range COCO-AP comparison \cite{sarsam2025}.

This line of work varies the architecture under a fixed from-scratch protocol. Multi-stage pretraining has been studied for box-level SAR detection on SARDet-100K \cite{sardet100k2024}. No SAR ship instance segmentation method isolates the pretraining regime as a variable, however, and none studies how the outputs of a trained model should be aggregated at inference. Under our protocol, COCO initialization moves the same Mask2Former from the reported 17.9 to 27.7 inshore mask AP, and we study recovery on that strong base instead of a new architecture.

\subsection{Test-Time Augmentation, Weight Ensembling, and Inference-Side Recovery}
\label{sec:related-tta}
Weight averaging is standard practice in detection: exponential moving average is a routine training configuration in modern detectors \cite{rtdetrv22024}, with reported gains of up to +2.5 AP in from-scratch training but only +0.2 to +0.6 when fine-tuning strong checkpoints \cite{switchema2024}; model soups average the weights of several models fine-tuned from a shared initialization \cite{modelsoups2022}; and a recent empirical study of weight averaging concentrates its experiments on from-scratch regimes \cite{weightavg2025}. Multi-scale flip test-time augmentation (TTA) is equally common practice, and the rule that merges its outputs has been studied in box space. Soft-NMS decays overlapping scores instead of deleting them \cite{softnms2017}, weighted boxes fusion averages the coordinates of clustered boxes \cite{wbf2021}, and a learned aggregator weights augmented predictions for image classification \cite{shanmugam2021}. Mask Scoring R-CNN \cite{msrcnn2019} and IoU-Net \cite{iounet2018} learn a mask- or box-IoU head inside the detector and rescore with it; our re-ranker regresses the same mask-IoU target outside the frozen model from cross-view consensus features. On the output side, BPR crops patches along predicted instance contours and re-segments them with a small high-resolution network as a model-agnostic post-processing stage \cite{bpr2021}. PointRend \cite{pointrend2020} trains a point-wise head jointly with the segmentation network, and SegFix \cite{segfix2020} learns boundary offsets from the image and ground truth alone. BPR is adopted because it is the only one of the three fitted on a frozen model's own coarse masks. DiffusionDet makes the proposal count and the number of denoising steps an evaluation-time dial on an already-trained model, 45.8 to 46.8 box AP on COCO \cite{diffusiondet2023}.

Two questions remain open in this family. The weight-averaging evidence is thinnest exactly where fine-tuning starts from a strong checkpoint. Beyond box-space NMS and weighted boxes fusion, no study asks which operator should merge the dozens of overlapping mask-level hypotheses that wide TTA produces. Section~IV shows that the merge rule, not the view count, sets the outcome of aggressive TTA.

\subsection{Adaptation and Priors on Strong Bases}
\label{sec:related-peft}
\label{sec:related-aniso}
A growing adapter literature argues that strong pretrained representations should be preserved, not overwritten. Mona shows that a multi-cognitive convolutional adapter surpasses full fine-tuning on COCO instance segmentation by +0.9 mask AP, whereas the linear low-rank adapter LoRand falls 0.4 AP below full fine-tuning (44.7 versus 45.1) \cite{mona2025}, so both preservation and the adapter's inductive content matter; Frozen-DETR enriches a detector with tokens from a frozen vision foundation model and gains +2.9 AP on natural-image benchmarks \cite{frozendetr2024}; and LayerScale's small-initialized per-channel residual scaling informs how such adapters are initialized \cite{cait2021}. A parallel line injects anisotropic priors for elongated targets: SegNeXt employs multiplicative strip attention \cite{segnext2022}, InceptionNeXt uses parallel strip kernels with a band-kernel sweet spot near $k=11$ \cite{inceptionnext2024}, Strip R-CNN applies sequential $1\times k$/$k\times 1$ strip convolutions to remote-sensing detection \cite{striprcnn2026}, and in SAR the orientation-aware module of O2Former encodes anisotropy directly \cite{o2former2025}.

Preservation, however, proves necessary but not sufficient: on a COCO-pretrained Mask2Former, none of seven controlled architecture- and training-side arms paid, and the strip prior transferred at $-0.9$ inshore versus +0.6 offshore mask AP (Section~IV). A plausible prior fails under regime shift, so our cascade recovers accuracy without modifying the base model at all.
