\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{SAR Ship Instance Segmentation}
\label{sec:related-sar}
Published SAR ship instance segmentation methods are predominantly specialized architectures trained from scratch, that is, an ImageNet-pretrained backbone with randomly initialized detection and mask heads, under long schedules on HRSID and SSDD. LFG-Net exploits low-level feature guidance with super-resolution denoising to counter the scattering and noise characteristics of near-shore scenes \cite{lfgnet2022}, a bounding-box-free Gaussian-heatmap formulation with centroid- and orientation-constrained losses reports 42.5 inshore mask AP on HRSID \cite{gaussheatmap2024}, and DiffSARShipInst couples a diffusion-based instance decoder with aspect-ratio-matched asymmetric-convolution mask refinement and reports 42.6 inshore \cite{diffsar2025}, although its published figures circulate through secondary tables and have, to our knowledge, not been independently reproduced. O2Former holds the strongest split-matched inshore result to date, 43.1 mask AP, by augmenting Mask2Former \cite{mask2former2022} with an optimized query generator and an orientation-aware polar-coordinate module under a 500-epoch from-scratch protocol in which its own Mask2Former baseline reaches only 17.9 inshore \cite{o2former2025}. SARSAM instead adapts the Segment Anything Model by parameter-efficient tuning, but reports only AP at IoU 0.50 (90.12 on the full HRSID test set), which precludes full-range COCO-AP comparison \cite{sarsam2025}. No published method isolates the pretraining regime as a variable. We show that COCO initialization alone moves the same Mask2Former from 17.9 to 27.7 inshore mask AP, and we study training-free recovery on that strong base rather than a new architecture.

\subsection{Parameter-Efficient Adaptation on Strong Bases}
\label{sec:related-peft}
A growing adapter literature argues that strong pretrained representations should be preserved rather than overwritten. Mona shows that a multi-cognitive convolutional adapter surpasses full fine-tuning on COCO instance segmentation by +0.9 mask AP with a Swin-B Cascade Mask R-CNN, whereas the linear low-rank adapter LoRand falls 0.4 AP below full fine-tuning (44.7 versus 45.1), so both preservation and the adapter's inductive content matter \cite{mona2025}. Frozen-DETR likewise enriches a detector with tokens from a frozen vision foundation model and gains +2.9 AP on natural-image benchmarks \cite{frozendetr2024}. Our findings sharpen this principle for SAR: on a COCO-pretrained Mask2Former, seven controlled architecture- and training-side arms, including a zero-initialized gated convolutional adapter that preserves the pretrained function exactly at the start of fine-tuning, were flat, negative, or insufficient (Section~IV). Preservation alone does not guarantee a gain. The injected prior must also match the target regime (Section~\ref{sec:related-aniso}).

\subsection{Test-Time Augmentation and Weight Ensembling}
\label{sec:related-tta}
Weight averaging is standard practice in detection: exponential moving average is a routine training configuration in modern detectors \cite{rtdetrv22024}, with reported gains of up to +2.5 AP in from-scratch training but only +0.2 to +0.6 when fine-tuning strong checkpoints \cite{switchema2024}; model soups average the weights of several models fine-tuned from a shared initialization \cite{modelsoups2022}; and a recent empirical study of weight averaging concentrates its experiments on from-scratch regimes \cite{weightavg2025}, leaving fine-tuning from strong checkpoints under-quantified. Multi-scale flip test-time augmentation (TTA) is commonly credited with +0.5 to +1.5 AP on COCO, whereas we observe +6.9 inshore mask AP from a six-view ensemble. On the output side, BPR crops patches along predicted instance contours and re-segments them with a small high-resolution network as a model-agnostic post-processing stage \cite{bpr2021}; we adopt this paradigm as an optional 2.35M-parameter second tier and audit it for ground-truth leakage (Section~IV). Re-ranking the outputs of a frozen detector belongs to the same inference-side family, and its nearest precedent in detection is DiffusionDet, which turns the number of proposal boxes and denoising steps into an evaluation-time dial that buys accuracy with computation on an already-trained model, 45.8 to 46.8 box AP on COCO \cite{diffusiondet2023}. That dial spends extra compute on generating candidates; our third tier spends it on adjudicating them, learning a mask-quality score from the consensus statistics that the multi-view stack produces anyway and re-ordering detections whose masks it never touches. What this literature leaves open is the fusion question. Our ablations show that the merge operator, not the view count, decides whether aggressive TTA helps or collapses, and that a same-basin weight soup matches a three-seed prediction ensemble at one third of the inference cost.

\subsection{Anisotropic Priors}
\label{sec:related-aniso}
Elongated targets have motivated strip-shaped operators. SegNeXt employs multiplicative strip attention \cite{segnext2022}, InceptionNeXt uses parallel strip kernels with a band-kernel sweet spot near $k=11$ \cite{inceptionnext2024}, and Strip R-CNN applies sequential $1\times k$/$k\times 1$ strip convolutions to remote-sensing detection \cite{striprcnn2026}. Learnable per-channel residual scaling with small initialization, introduced as LayerScale for deep transformers \cite{cait2021}, informs the initialization of such adapters, and in SAR the orientation-aware module of O2Former encodes anisotropy directly \cite{o2former2025}. Our controlled test found that the strip prior transfers negatively to the inshore split ($-0.9$ mask AP) and marginally positively offshore (+0.6): small near-shore HRSID ships are quasi point-like, so a plausible elongation prior fails under regime shift. We report this negative transfer as controlled evidence rather than discard it.
