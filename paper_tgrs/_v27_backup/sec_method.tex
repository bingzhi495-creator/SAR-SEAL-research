\section{Method}\label{sec:method}

This section presents the proposed framework. We first fix notation and the problem setup, then describe the base model and the pretraining regime on which every later number rests. The three training-free recovery operators follow in pipeline order: a same-basin weight soup in weight space (Sec.~\ref{sec:soup}), wide multi-scale flip test-time augmentation (TTA) in input space (Sec.~\ref{sec:tta}), and a consensus-gated mask-IoU vote fusion in output space (Sec.~\ref{sec:fusion}). We close with two optional self-correctors, a boundary-patch refiner that may edit only mask pixels (Sec.~\ref{sec:bpr}) and a learned consensus re-ranker that may edit only scores (Sec.~\ref{sec:rerank}), and with an analysis of computational cost (Sec.~\ref{sec:complexity}). Fig.~\ref{fig:framework} summarizes the pipeline. The figures of this section draw measured tensors rather than sketches, and three conventions hold across all of them. Every tensor is drawn to one geometric scale, $1$\,mm of printed edge per $40$ image pixels and $+0.55$\,mm of slab depth per channel doubling; the only exception is the $2048@25^2$ backbone slab of Fig.~\ref{fig:framework}, drawn at a $1.2$\,mm legibility floor instead of its true $0.63$\,mm and marked $\approx$. At that scale the median HRSID inshore ship, $0.028\%$ of an $800\times800$ chip or $13.4$\,px across, measures $0.33$\,mm, which is why the boundary crops of Fig.~\ref{fig:m-bpr} are shown in explicit zooms. Feature maps are rendered as the $L_2$ norm over channels in \emph{viridis} and are upsampled by nearest neighbor, so no spatial detail is invented, and the provenance of every drawn chip, tensor, and matrix is recorded in \texttt{figs/assets/manifest.json}.

\subsection{Problem Setup and Notation}\label{sec:setup}

Given a single-look SAR amplitude chip $\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times 3\times 800\times 800}$, in which the grayscale channel is replicated to three channels and $B=1$ at test time, ship instance segmentation asks for a set of scored binary masks
\begin{equation}\label{eq:output}
\mathcal{Y}=\bigl\{(\mathbf{m}_i,s_i)\bigr\}_{i=1}^{N_{\mathrm{out}}},\quad
\mathbf{m}_i\in\{0,1\}^{800\times 800},\ s_i\in[0,1],
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{m}_i$ is the binary mask of the $i$th instance, $s_i$ its confidence, and $N_{\mathrm{out}}\le 100$ after score ranking under the COCO convention. Evaluation is COCO mask AP ($\times$100 throughout) on the HRSID official test split and on its inshore and offshore partitions, following the protocol of \cite{o2former2025}. Tensors are ordered $B\times C\times H\times W$; bold lower case denotes vectors or masks, bold upper case matrices, calligraphic letters sets, and $\theta$ the full parameter vector of the segmentation network $\Phi_{\theta}$. The method has two stages, and its inference side is a cascade of three rungs. Stage~A is a standard, unmodified Mask2Former-R50 \cite{mask2former2022} fine-tuned from COCO initialization. Stage~B, our contribution, updates no weight of Stage~A. Its first rung is training-free and acts at three disjoint pipeline stages, weight space, input space, and output-fusion space. Its second and third rungs are two small correctors fitted on Stage~A's own errors on the training split rather than on new annotations: a boundary-patch refiner that may write only mask pixels (Sec.~\ref{sec:bpr}) and a consensus re-ranker that may write only scores (Sec.~\ref{sec:rerank}). Every rung is optional, every rung is reported separately in each table, and the training-free claim of this article is scoped to the first.

\subsection{Base Model and the Pretraining Regime}\label{sec:base}

The base is used exactly as released. The ResNet-50 backbone produces a four-level pyramid $\{C_2,C_3,C_4,C_5\}$ at strides $\{4,8,16,32\}$, with shapes $B\times 256\times 200\times 200$, $B\times 512\times 100\times 100$, $B\times 1024\times 50\times 50$, and $B\times 2048\times 25\times 25$ for an $800\times 800$ input. A six-layer multi-scale deformable-attention pixel decoder (8 heads, 256 dimensions) fuses $C_3$--$C_5$ into a stride-4 mask feature $\mathbf{F}\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times 256\times 200\times 200}$ plus three flattened memory levels at strides $\{8,16,32\}$. $N=100$ learnable queries $\mathbf{Q}\in\mathbb{R}^{B\times 100\times 256}$ traverse nine decoder layers, each applying masked cross-attention to one memory level in round-robin order, query self-attention, and a feed-forward block. A linear head maps each query to class logits in $\mathbb{R}^{B\times 100\times 2}$ (ship versus no-object); a mask head maps it to an embedding $\mathbf{e}_q\in\mathbb{R}^{256}$, and the mask of query $q$ is read out as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:readout}
\mathbf{m}_q=\mathbf{1}\Bigl[\sigma\bigl(\mathcal{U}_{4\times}(\mathbf{e}_q^{\top}\mathbf{F})\bigr)\ge 0.5\Bigr],
\qquad q=1,\dots,100,
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{e}_q^{\top}\mathbf{F}\in\mathbb{R}^{200\times 200}$ is the per-pixel dot product between the mask embedding and $\mathbf{F}$, $\mathcal{U}_{4\times}$ denotes bilinear upsampling to $800\times 800$, $\sigma$ is the sigmoid, and $\mathbf{1}[\cdot]$ is the indicator applied pixelwise.

Stage~A fine-tunes this model from COCO instance-segmentation weights for 55 epochs (100{,}872 iterations at batch size 2 with 2-step gradient accumulation) at $800\times 800$ resolution and initial learning rate $2.5\times 10^{-5}$ on a single RTX 3090\,Ti. An auxiliary mask-IoU rescoring head present in the implementation is disabled ($\lambda=0$); the weight soup subsumes its gain (Section~IV). The initialization is not a detail. Under the 500-epoch from-scratch-head protocol of \cite{o2former2025}, the identical architecture reaches 17.9 inshore mask AP; with COCO initialization it reaches 27.7 (three-seed mean) after 55 epochs. This 9.8-point regime gap exceeds any single architectural increment reported in the specialized SAR instance-segmentation literature. We therefore impose a preservation constraint: no layer, loss, or training schedule of Stage~A is modified. Section~IV supports this constraint empirically with a seven-direction ablation in which every architecture- and training-side modification of this base was flat, negative, or absorbed.

\subsection{Same-Basin Weight Soup}\label{sec:soup}

Fine-tuning is stochastic. Three independently seeded runs land within 0.1 inshore AP of one another (27.7/27.6/27.7), yet their prediction errors differ, and the first operator removes this seed variance in weight space. The three checkpoints are averaged elementwise into a uniform model soup \cite{modelsoups2022},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:soup}
\theta^{\star}=\frac{1}{3}\sum_{s\in\{s_0,\,s_2,\,s_3\}}\theta_s,
\end{equation}
where $\theta_{s_0}$, $\theta_{s_2}$, and $\theta_{s_3}$ are the parameter vectors of the three fine-tuned checkpoints. Two same-basin conditions make the average a flat-minimum model rather than a broken interpolation: all members are fine-tuned from the same COCO initialization with identical parameter keys, and their batch-normalization buffers are byte-equal. Fig.~\ref{fig:m-soup} draws the measured geometry: over all 565 floating-point tensors (44.2\,M parameters) of the three checkpoints the pairwise distances $\|\theta_{s_i}-\theta_{s_j}\|_2$ are 30.16, 29.81, and 30.18, so the seeds sit at almost exactly equal separations, and $\theta^{\star}$ is their centroid at 17.27, 17.49, and 17.29 from them; the released checkpoint reproduces the recomputed uniform mean to $1.6\times 10^{-7}$, that is, to float32 rounding. These distances are recomputed by \texttt{figs/measure\_soup\_geometry.py} into \texttt{figs/assets/soup\_geometry.json}. The soup is computed once, offline, and yields a single deterministic checkpoint at $1\times$ inference cost. It lifts the three-seed mean from 27.7 to 29.6 inshore (+1.9), 45.6 to 47.4 official, and 59.6 to 61.1 offshore. Under the full stack this single checkpoint matches a three-model prediction ensemble (39.3/53.6/64.0) at one third of its inference cost (Section~IV).

\subsection{Wide Multi-Scale Flip TTA}\label{sec:tta}

The second operator attacks scale-induced variance in input space. The souped model $\Phi_{\theta^{\star}}$ is run under the view set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:views}
\mathcal{V}=\{640,\,800,\,960,\,1088\}\times\{\mathrm{id},\,\mathrm{hflip}\},
\qquad V=|\mathcal{V}|=8,
\end{equation}
where each pair $(\rho,f)\in\mathcal{V}$ resizes the chip to $\rho\times\rho$ and optionally applies a horizontal flip. The pooled candidate set is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pool}
\bigl\{(\mathbf{m}_k,s_k)\bigr\}_{k=1}^{K}
=\bigcup_{(\rho,f)\in\mathcal{V}}
f^{-1}\Bigl(\Phi_{\theta^{\star}}\bigl(f(R_{\rho}(\mathbf{x}))\bigr)\Bigr),
\end{equation}
where $R_{\rho}$ denotes bilinear resizing to $\rho\times\rho$, $f^{-1}$ inverts the flip on the predicted masks (the identity for unflipped views), and the mask head rescales its outputs (\texttt{rescale=True}) so that every view returns masks at the native $800\times 800$ resolution; consequently $K\le 8\times 100=800$ scored candidates per image. Fig.~\ref{fig:m-tta} depicts this fan-out and pooling geometry.

\begin{figure*}[!t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figs/figm_tta}
\caption{Wide multi-scale flip TTA. (a)~The eight views of Eq.~(\ref{eq:views}) are the real rasters computed on HRSID inshore chip \texttt{P0101\_1200\_2000\_3000\_3800.jpg}, drawn at their true relative size of 16.0/20.0/24.0/27.2\,mm, so the 1088 view places $2.9\times$ the pixels of the 640 view on the same hull. (b)~The same eight outputs, first on their four native rasters and then on one common $800\times800$ raster after $f^{-1}$ and rescaling. (c)~Measured mask AP gained by the six-view core over the single checkpoint, three-seed mean.}

The set is deliberately wide. A six-view core $\{736,800,864\}\times\{\mathrm{id},\mathrm{hflip}\}$ already lifts single-checkpoint inshore AP by +7.0 on average (+6.9/+7.4/+6.7 across the three seeds, $\sigma\approx 0.3$), with a regime gradient (inshore +7.0, official +5.5, offshore +3.4) concentrated exactly where speckle and dock clutter are hardest. Widening to the eight views of Eq.~(\ref{eq:views}) adds a further +2.0 inshore on seed $s_0$ (34.6 to 36.6) yet is flat on the official split and costs 0.6 offshore: the upsampled scales 960 and 1088 place more pixels on pixel-starved near-shore hulls, while large, well-resolved offshore ships gain nothing. The soup and TTA axes stack near-additively; their standalone inshore gains (+1.9 and +6.9) predict 36.5 where the measured combination reaches 36.1, a mildly sub-additive interaction, and we claim no synergy. What the pooled views do demand is a fusion rule. Under plain box-NMS merging the soup $\times$ 8-view configuration reaches 37.1/52.6/64.2 inshore/official/offshore; the next subsection shows why this reference fuser underuses the ensemble and how two plausible alternatives fail outright.

\subsection{Consensus-Gated Mask-IoU Vote Fusion}\label{sec:fusion}

Merging up to 800 overlapping candidates is where aggressive TTA either pays or collapses, and the observed failure modes dictate the design. Box-space suppression is geometrically wrong for harbors: two ships berthed in parallel produce boxes with high IoU but masks with low IoU, so box-NMS structurally deletes true neighbors. Score-decay suppression fails differently. SOLO-style matrix-NMS only attenuates the roughly eight near-duplicates that $V=8$ views generate per object, floods the ranking with survivors, and collapses to 4.1/8.9/14.4 mask AP on the identical candidate set. Unconditional pixelwise averaging fails on the opposite flank: majority voting over every cluster erodes long, well-resolved offshore boundaries to 55.9, below the 59.6 no-TTA base, even though the same candidates reach 64.2 offshore under box-NMS. The fuser, not the views, is the safety condition. Our operator therefore clusters in mask-IoU space, deletes duplicates hard, and averages only where the ensemble itself signals disagreement.

All $K$ candidate masks are flattened into rows of $\mathbf{M}\in\{0,1\}^{K\times HW}$, and the pairwise mask-IoU (Jaccard) matrix is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:iou}
J_{jk}=\frac{\mathbf{m}_j^{\top}\mathbf{m}_k}
{\mathbf{1}^{\top}\mathbf{m}_j+\mathbf{1}^{\top}\mathbf{m}_k-\mathbf{m}_j^{\top}\mathbf{m}_k},
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{m}_j^{\top}\mathbf{m}_k$, the $(j,k)$ entry of the Gram matrix $\mathbf{M}\mathbf{M}^{\top}$, counts intersection pixels and $\mathbf{1}^{\top}\mathbf{m}_j$ is the area of mask $j$. Candidates are consumed greedily in score order. With $\mathcal{U}$ the set of not-yet-consumed indices, one cluster is formed per iteration as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cluster}
\mathcal{C}=\{r\}\cup\bigl\{j\in\mathcal{U}: c_j=c_r,\ J_{rj}\ge\tau\bigr\},
\qquad r=\arg\max_{k\in\mathcal{U}}s_k,
\end{equation}
where $r$ indexes the seed (the highest-scoring unconsumed candidate), $c_j$ is the class label of candidate $j$, and $\tau=0.5$ is the mask-IoU threshold. Each cluster is read out under a cross-view agreement gate,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fuse}
\mathbf{m}^{\mathrm{out}}(p)=
\begin{cases}
\mathbf{1}\Bigl[\displaystyle\sum_{j\in\mathcal{C}}\mathbf{m}_j(p)>\tfrac{|\mathcal{C}|}{2}\Bigr],
& 2\le|\mathcal{C}|\le S_{\max},\\[8pt]
\mathbf{m}_r(p), & \text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
where $p$ ranges over pixels and $S_{\max}=6$ bounds the agreement band. The output score is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:score}
s^{\mathrm{out}}=s_r\,\bigl(|\mathcal{C}|/V\bigr)^{\gamma},
\qquad \gamma=0,
\end{equation}
where $\gamma=0$ reduces the rule to plain seed-score inheritance; the general form is kept because the consensus ratio $|\mathcal{C}|/V$ is the quantity the gate consumes. The output box is regenerated from the fused mask. Algorithm~\ref{alg:fusion} lists the complete procedure, and Fig.~\ref{fig:m-fusion} shows the operator on a real candidate pool, together with the two failure modes that motivate its gate. The clusters it produces are disjoint in practice: on the eight-view pool of one inshore chip the 16 highest-scoring candidates fall into four clusters of sizes 6, 7, 1, and 2, and every off-block entry of the mask-IoU matrix, recorded in \texttt{figs/assets/iou\_matrix\_meta.json}, is exactly zero.

\begin{algorithm}[!t]
\caption{Consensus-Gated Mask-IoU Vote Fusion}
\label{alg:fusion}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE candidates $\{(\mathbf{m}_k,s_k)\}_{k=1}^{K}$ from $V=8$ views; mask-IoU threshold $\tau=0.5$; agreement band $[2,S_{\max}]$ with $S_{\max}=6$; $\gamma=0$
\ENSURE fused instance set $\mathcal{Y}$
\STATE sort candidates by score, descending; mark all unused; $\mathcal{Y}\leftarrow\emptyset$
\WHILE{unused candidates remain}
  \STATE $r\leftarrow$ highest-scoring unused candidate; $\mathcal{C}\leftarrow\{r\}$
  \STATE absorb every unused same-class $j$ with $J_{rj}\ge\tau$ into $\mathcal{C}$
  \STATE mark $\mathcal{C}$ used
  \IF{$2\le|\mathcal{C}|\le S_{\max}$}
    \STATE $\mathbf{m}^{\mathrm{out}}\leftarrow$ pixelwise majority vote over $\mathcal{C}$ \COMMENT{denoise}
  \ELSE
    \STATE $\mathbf{m}^{\mathrm{out}}\leftarrow\mathbf{m}_r$ \COMMENT{singleton or near-unanimous: preserve seed verbatim}
  \ENDIF
  \STATE $s^{\mathrm{out}}\leftarrow s_r\,(|\mathcal{C}|/V)^{\gamma}$; box $\leftarrow$ \texttt{mask2bbox}$(\mathbf{m}^{\mathrm{out}})$
  \STATE append $(\mathbf{m}^{\mathrm{out}},s^{\mathrm{out}})$ to $\mathcal{Y}$
\ENDWHILE
\RETURN top-100 of $\mathcal{Y}$ by score
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The agreement band reads the ensemble's own cross-view consensus as a per-instance variance estimate. A near-unanimous cluster ($|\mathcal{C}|>6$) is a clean detection whose seed mask any averaging can only erode; it is emitted verbatim. A singleton has nothing to vote over and is likewise preserved. Clusters inside the band exhibit genuine cross-view disagreement, typically ambiguous inshore ships, and only these are majority-denoised. The gate is monotone in the band ceiling (inshore 38.4/38.6/38.8 for $S_{\max}=4/5/6$, with offshore fixed at 64.8), and the full operator reaches 38.8/53.8/64.8, matching or exceeding the seed-only readout (38.2/53.5/64.8) on all three splits. Composed, the stack lifts the 27.7/45.6/59.6 base by +11.1/+8.2/+5.2 with no weight updated.

\subsection{Optional Boundary-Patch Refinement (BPR)}\label{sec:bpr}

The stack recovers detection and recall, not boundary precision. Mask logits predicted at stride 4 ($200\times 200$) are upsampled $4\times$ bilinearly in Eq.~(\ref{eq:readout}), so predicted contours carry a soft halo of roughly one to two pixels, and on a typical $9\times 18$-pixel inshore hull a one-pixel boundary shift already costs about 0.15--0.25 IoU. No training-free control we tested moves this residual (Section~IV). We therefore add an optional trained second rung in the BPR paradigm \cite{bpr2021}, constructed so that it cannot alter anything except boundary pixels; Fig.~\ref{fig:m-bpr} summarizes its training and band-guarded application paths.

\begin{figure*}[!t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{figs/figm_bpr}
\caption{Boundary-patch refinement on the real fused instance masks of HRSID inshore chip \texttt{P0101\_1200\_2000\_3000\_3800.jpg}. (a)~Training: the vermillion box is the real patch window at origin $(536,0)$, $1.6$\,mm against the $20$\,mm mask and hence shown at $\times8$; inside the zoom it appears before and after refinement, red contour coarse and green refined. (b)~Inference: the vermillion box is the real $57\times73$\,px band crop and the $\times12$ zoom shows the annulus $\mathrm{dilate}(m,4)\ominus\mathrm{erode}(m,4)$, in which amber is the only editable region; padlocks mark its interior and its exterior.}
\label{fig:m-bpr}
\end{figure*}

The refiner is trained once, in about 19 minutes. The full stack is run on the training split and its predictions are matched to training ground truth at mask IoU $\ge 0.5$; square boundary patches of side 64, upscaled $2\times$, are cropped along each matched contour, forming (prediction, ground-truth) patch pairs. A 2.35M-parameter UNet is trained on these pairs with a combined binary cross-entropy and Dice objective,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bprloss}
\begin{split}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{ref}}
={}&-\frac{1}{|\Omega|}\sum_{p\in\Omega}\bigl[y_p\log\hat{y}_p+(1-y_p)\log(1-\hat{y}_p)\bigr]\\
&+1-\frac{2\sum_{p\in\Omega}\hat{y}_p\,y_p+\varepsilon}
{\sum_{p\in\Omega}\hat{y}_p+\sum_{p\in\Omega}y_p+\varepsilon},
\end{split}
\end{equation}
where $\Omega$ is the set of patch pixels, $\hat{y}_p\in(0,1)$ the refiner's sigmoid output at pixel $p$, $y_p\in\{0,1\}$ the ground-truth label, and $\varepsilon$ a small stability constant. The main model is never touched, and the filename overlap between refiner training patches and test images is zero.

Application is band-guarded. At test time the refiner re-segments the boundary patches of each detected instance, and the refined prediction is written back only inside a $\pm 4$-pixel band around the original boundary,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:guard}
\begin{split}
\mathbf{m}^{\mathrm{final}}(p)&=b(p)\,\hat{\mathbf{m}}(p)+\bigl(1-b(p)\bigr)\,\mathbf{m}^{\mathrm{out}}(p),\\
b(p)&=\mathbf{1}\bigl[d\bigl(p,\partial\mathbf{m}^{\mathrm{out}}\bigr)\le 4\bigr],
\end{split}
\end{equation}
where $\hat{\mathbf{m}}$ is the refiner output stitched over the patches, $\partial\mathbf{m}^{\mathrm{out}}$ the boundary of the stack's mask, and $d(\cdot,\cdot)$ the pixel distance to that boundary. Overlapping patch logits are averaged inside the band and thresholded at 0.5. The resulting edit is small as well as confined: in the instance drawn in Fig.~\ref{fig:m-bpr}, refinement flips 106 of the 4096 pixels of one boundary patch, and the $\pm 4$-pixel band around a 738-pixel hull holds 1157 editable pixels. Every pixel outside the band is bit-identical to the stack's output, and detection scores, ranks, and instance identities are untouched: the refiner can displace boundaries by a few pixels but can neither add, remove, nor re-rank detections. With this rung the pipeline reaches 43.6/58.6/69.1 mask AP and 47.0/70.7/87.4 AP$_{75}$ on inshore/official/offshore. The metric fingerprint is that of a pure boundary correction, since inshore AP$_{50}$ moves by only +0.7 while AP$_{75}$ moves by +10.9; Section~IV audits the application path along seven leakage vectors. The training-free claim of this article is accordingly scoped to the stack of Secs.~\ref{sec:soup}--\ref{sec:fusion}, and the refiner, like the re-ranker that follows, is reported separately in every table.

\subsection{Learned Consensus Re-Ranking}\label{sec:rerank}

Two axes of the residual are now treated and a third is not. Mask AP is a ranking metric, so a candidate whose mask is accurate but whose score falls below a worse neighbor forfeits precision at every recall level it should have dominated. The fusion operator of Sec.~\ref{sec:fusion} already computes the quantity that predicts mask quality, the cross-view consensus of each cluster, and then discards it: Eq.~(\ref{eq:score}) reduces to seed-score inheritance at $\gamma=0$ because no fixed exponent on $|\mathcal{C}|/V$ improved all three splits at once. Rather than search harder for a formula, we stop writing one. The base model makes its errors on the training split as readily as on the test split, and those errors are free supervision, so the map from consensus evidence to mask quality is fitted instead of guessed. Fig.~\ref{fig:m-rerank} summarizes the operator.

\begin{figure}[!t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\columnwidth]{figs/figm_rerank}
\caption{The learned consensus re-ranker. (a)~Each fused instance is described by the 14 scalars of Eqs.~(\ref{eq:rrfeat})--(\ref{eq:rrblocks}): six consensus statistics (vermillion), the score the fusion stage emits (gray), and seven mask-geometry descriptors (blue). (b)~Measured permutation importance of the three feature groups. (c)~Only the score field is rewritten: the two instance-mask rasters are byte-identical files, and only the height of the score bar changes.}
\label{fig:m-rerank}
\end{figure}

Each fused instance is described by a 14-dimensional vector of scalars,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rrfeat}
\boldsymbol{\phi}=\bigl[\boldsymbol{\phi}_{\mathrm{c}}^{\top},\ s^{\mathrm{out}},\ \boldsymbol{\phi}_{\mathrm{g}}^{\top}\bigr]^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{14},
\end{equation}
whose consensus and geometry blocks are
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rrblocks}
\begin{split}
\boldsymbol{\phi}_{\mathrm{c}}&=\bigl[\,|\mathcal{C}|,\ n_v,\ \bar{J},\ J_{\min},\ s_r,\ \Delta s\,\bigr]^{\top},\\
\boldsymbol{\phi}_{\mathrm{g}}&=\bigl[\,\log(1{+}a),\ \log(1{+}w),\ \log(1{+}h),\\
&\qquad\quad w/h,\ a/(wh),\ c_x,\ c_y\,\bigr]^{\top},
\end{split}
\end{equation}
where $|\mathcal{C}|$ is the cluster size of Eq.~(\ref{eq:cluster}), $n_v$ the number of distinct views of Eq.~(\ref{eq:views}) contributing to that cluster, $\bar{J}$ and $J_{\min}$ the mean and the minimum pairwise mask IoU of Eq.~(\ref{eq:iou}) inside it, $s_r$ the seed score, $\Delta s$ the spread between the largest and the smallest member score, $s^{\mathrm{out}}$ the score the fusion stage emits, $a$ the mask area, $w$ and $h$ the width and height of the mask box, and $(c_x,c_y)$ the box center normalized by image width and height. Under the deployed setting $\gamma=0$ the two score channels $s_r$ and $s^{\mathrm{out}}$ coincide, which costs a tree ensemble nothing and keeps the same extractor valid for $\gamma>0$. No image pixel is read anywhere in Eq.~(\ref{eq:rrblocks}): every entry is a by-product of computation the stack has already performed, which is what makes the operator cost microseconds per instance.

The regression target is the mask IoU between the candidate and its best-matching training annotation, and zero when the candidate overlaps none. Every candidate above a score floor of 0.01 contributes a row, kept and rejected alike, so the model sees the failures and not only the survivors; HRSID yields 51{,}781 rows from 3{,}642 training images and PSeg-SSDD 5{,}122 rows from 928. A histogram-based gradient-boosted regression tree $g$ (300 boosting iterations, learning rate 0.08, least-squares loss) is fitted to these rows, and its prediction is blended into the detection score as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:rrblend}
s^{\mathrm{final}}=\alpha\,\mathrm{clip}\bigl(g(\boldsymbol{\phi}),0,1\bigr)+(1-\alpha)\,s^{\mathrm{out}},
\end{equation}
where $\alpha\in[0,1]$ trades the learned quality estimate against the network's own confidence and the clip keeps the regressor inside the score range.

The mixing weight is the only hyperparameter, and it is selected without touching test data. Training images are partitioned by image identifier into a fit part and a holdout part in a 4:1 ratio (2{,}914 and 728 images on HRSID), $g$ is fitted on the fit part alone, and $\alpha$ is swept over $\{0.3,0.5,1.0\}$ by COCO segm AP measured on the held-out training images. HRSID selects $\alpha=0.5$, which moves that train-side proxy from 57.6 to 58.9 AP; PSeg-SSDD, whose re-ranker is refitted from scratch on its own training split, selects $\alpha=1.0$ and moves its proxy from 65.0 to 67.8. The chosen weight is then frozen and $g$ is refitted on all training rows. Everything runs on a CPU: 4 s to harvest features and 19 s to fit on HRSID, 2 s and 9 s on PSeg-SSDD. On the held-out training images the Spearman correlation between $g$ and the true mask IoU is 0.68 on HRSID and 0.86 on PSeg-SSDD, and permutation importance ranks the score channel first by a wide margin, then mask area, fill ratio, and the view count $n_v$. Grouped by feature block over the held-out 20\% of the 3\,642 HRSID training images, with three repeats, that importance reads 1.1418 for the six consensus statistics, 0.0771 for the seven geometry descriptors, and 0.0000 for the emitted score, the seed score alone accounting for 1.0944 of the consensus total (Fig.~\ref{fig:m-rerank}); the measurement is reproduced by \texttt{figs/measure\_rerank\_importance.py}. The operator does not replace the network's confidence; it corrects it with evidence the network never saw in a single forward pass.

Two safety properties follow from what the operator is allowed to write. First, the candidate set is preserved exactly. Masks, instance identities, and the size of the output set are bit-identical to the input, and only the score field changes, so re-ranking can neither create, delete, nor reshape a detection. Each detection of the preceding rung is bound to its fusion-stage candidate by a greedy one-to-one mask-IoU match at threshold 0.5, purely to retrieve that candidate's consensus statistics, and a detection with no match keeps its original score untouched; on the HRSID inshore split 23{,}857 of 28{,}539 detections are matched. Second, the refiner and the re-ranker write to disjoint fields. The refiner edits mask pixels inside a $\pm 4$-pixel band, the re-ranker reads scalars produced by the fusion stage and edits scores, so neither can undo the other and the order of application is a matter of convenience; we apply the refiner first so that the masks finally reported are the refined ones. On HRSID the re-ranking rung lifts 43.6/58.6/69.1 to 44.5/59.4/70.1 mask AP and inshore AP$_{75}$ from 47.0 to 48.3; on PSeg-SSDD it lifts 60.0/66.3/68.8 to 62.1/67.5/69.8. A carrier or a dataset new to the recipe needs its own $g$, since the consensus statistics belong to that carrier, and refitting one costs under a CPU-minute and no GPU time.

\subsection{Complexity}\label{sec:complexity}

The soup costs one offline elementwise average and nothing at inference; the recovery stack updates no weights and adds no parameters to the base. Test-time cost is dominated by the $V=8$ forward passes of Eq.~(\ref{eq:pool}), and the measured end-to-end cost of the full method is approximately $8\times$ a single-view pass. Fusion adds one $K\times K$ Gram product over binary masks and a greedy scan with $K\le 800$; this overhead is contained in the measured $8\times$ figure. The optional refiner adds a 2.35M-parameter UNet, trained once in about 19 minutes, and a per-instance boundary-patch pass at test time; total cost remains approximately $8\times$ plus this small constant. The optional re-ranker is cheaper still. Its features are already computed by the fusion stage, one gradient-boosted tree evaluation over 14 scalars costs microseconds per instance, and no image is read a second time, so the rung is free to the nearest measurable unit at test time and costs 23 s of CPU once at fit time on HRSID (11 s on PSeg-SSDD). Stage~A itself trains for 55 epochs on one GPU, against the 500-epoch specialized protocol of \cite{o2former2025}. No component of Stage~B touches a single weight of Stage~A, so the recipe wraps any Mask2Former-class base unchanged.
