\begin{abstract}
Deep learning methods have substantially advanced ship instance segmentation in synthetic aperture radar (SAR) imagery. However, the residual error concentrates in the near-shore regime, where small, low-contrast hulls are embedded in bright dock clutter and specialized methods lose 25 to 30 mask average precision (AP) points relative to their own offshore performance. This article advances that regime without any new architecture. On HRSID, the proposed pipeline reaches 44.5 inshore mask AP and 48.3 inshore AP$_{75}$, above the strongest published 500-epoch specialized architecture on both metrics after 55 epochs of training, while its training-free rung alone attains an inshore AP$_{50}$ of 75.6 against 69.5 for that architecture. The margins come from treating the pretraining regime, not the architecture, as the largest lever: the same Mask2Former scored 17.9 inshore AP from scratch but 27.7 from Common Objects in Context (COCO) initialization, whereas seven architecture- and training-side modifications of that base were flat, negative, or absorbed. We therefore leave the model untouched and build a three-rung inference cascade. The first rung is training-free and composes three operators at disjoint pipeline stages, a same-basin weight soup in weight space, wide multi-scale flip test-time augmentation (TTA) in input space, and a consensus-gated mask intersection-over-union (IoU) vote fusion in output space, lifting 27.7/45.6/59.6 to 38.8/53.8/64.8 mask AP on the inshore, official, and offshore splits at zero additional training and roughly $8\times$ inference cost. Controlled ablations identify the fusion operator, not the view count, as the enabler of aggressive TTA: the identical views collapse to 4.1 inshore AP under matrix non-maximum suppression (NMS) and erode offshore AP to 55.9 under pixelwise majority voting. The remaining two rungs are self-correctors fitted on the base model's own training-split errors and need no new annotation. A 2.35M-parameter boundary-patch refiner, trained in about 19 min and confined to a $\pm 4$-pixel band around predicted boundaries, raises inshore AP$_{75}$ from 36.1 to 47.0; a 14-feature gradient-boosted consensus re-ranker, fitted in 19 s, then reorders detections without editing a single mask pixel and carries the three splits from 43.6/58.6/69.1 to 44.5/59.4/70.1 mask AP. Across six further carriers spanning five architecture families, the complete recipe lifts every carrier on every split. Experiments on HRSID and PSeg-SSDD demonstrate the effectiveness and robustness of the proposed framework.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

\IEEEPARstart{W}{ith} its all-weather, day-and-night imaging capability, synthetic aperture radar (SAR) plays a key role in maritime surveillance, fisheries monitoring, and vessel traffic management. Ship instance segmentation extends detection from bounding boxes to pixel-accurate masks and thereby supplies the hull geometry that heading estimation, berthing analysis, and fine-grained recognition require. The release of pixel-annotated benchmarks such as HRSID \cite{hrsid2020} has made the task quantitatively comparable across methods, and deep segmentation networks now dominate the reported results.

Progress, however, splits between two regimes. Offshore, well-resolved ships on open water are handled adequately by generic instance segmentation models. Inshore, small hulls with low or even negative contrast are buried in bright, structured dock clutter under single-look speckle, and the specialized architectures published to date lose 25 to 30 mask average precision (AP) points relative to their own offshore performance, while classic detectors and from-scratch baselines lose more than 30. The open problem therefore lives almost entirely in the near-shore split.

This article shows that the near-shore split advances furthest without a new architecture. On HRSID, the proposed pipeline reaches 44.5 inshore mask AP and 48.3 inshore AP$_{75}$, above the strongest published 500-epoch specialized architecture (O2Former \cite{o2former2025}, 43.1 and 45.9) on both metrics after 55 epochs of training; its training-free tier alone attains an inshore AP$_{50}$ of 75.6 against O2Former's 69.5; and the composed pipeline lifts its own base by +16.8 inshore AP.

The prevailing response in the literature has been architectural specialization. O2Former augments Mask2Former with an optimized query generator and an orientation-aware polar-coordinate embedding module \cite{o2former2025}, DiffSARShipInst couples a diffusion-based instance decoder with asymmetric-convolution mask refinement \cite{diffsar2025}, a bounding-box-free formulation encodes each ship as an elliptical Gaussian heatmap under centroid- and orientation-constrained losses \cite{gaussheatmap2024}, LFG-Net routes low-level feature guidance through super-resolution denoising to counter near-shore scattering \cite{lfgnet2022}, and SARSAM transfers the Segment Anything Model to SAR by parameter-efficient tuning \cite{sarsam2025}. O2Former and the baselines re-tabulated in its benchmark share a common protocol, namely, 500-epoch training with an ImageNet backbone and a from-scratch segmentation head, and all of these works attribute their gains to new operators. None of them isolates the pretraining regime as a variable.

This article starts from a different measurement. Under the from-scratch-head protocol, plain Mask2Former \cite{mask2former2022} scores 17.9 inshore mask AP on HRSID \cite{o2former2025}; the identical architecture initialized from Common Objects in Context (COCO) instance segmentation weights reaches 27.7 after only 55 epochs (three-seed mean). The pretraining regime alone is worth 9.8 points inshore, more than any single architectural component ablated in the specialized literature.

On this strong base we then ran a controlled seven-direction architecture- and training-side sweep, and no direction paid. Constraining the deformable-attention sampling offsets cost 1.0 inshore point, one-to-many auxiliary supervision was flat, four training-side families (capacity, feature enrichment, data augmentation, and supervision) were each subsumed by the COCO-initialized base, and a mask intersection-over-union (IoU) rescoring head retained only a +0.1 residual once the weight soup was applied. A zero-gated strip adapter, built from sequential $1\times 7$/$7\times 1$ depthwise convolutions in line with current strip-operator guidance \cite{striprcnn2026,segnext2022,inceptionnext2024} and initialized to preserve the COCO weights exactly in the spirit of LayerScale \cite{cait2021}, cost 0.9 inshore point, and a Swin-S backbone swap \cite{swin2021} gained +0.9 at the base yet fell below the ResNet-50 configuration on all three splits once the full recovery stack was applied. This ledger echoes the preservation evidence from the adaptation literature, where adapters that protect strong pretrained representations outperform full fine-tuning \cite{mona2025} and frozen foundation features enrich detectors without being overwritten \cite{frozendetr2024}. The residual inshore error does not behave as a capacity problem.

If the residual is instead dominated by variance (which basin the short schedule lands in, which view of the scene the model sees, and how overlapping predictions are merged), then the matched remedy is training-free. We recover accuracy with three operators at three disjoint pipeline stages. In weight space, a same-basin soup averages three independently seeded checkpoints elementwise \cite{modelsoups2022} and adds +1.9 inshore at $1\times$ inference cost; reported weight-averaging gains concentrate in from-scratch regimes \cite{switchema2024,weightavg2025}, and fine-tuning from a strong checkpoint is exactly where that literature is thinnest. In input space, wide multi-scale flip test-time augmentation (TTA) over eight views places more pixels on pixel-starved near-shore hulls: the six-view core adds +6.9 inshore, robust across seeds, and widening the scale set to $\{640, 800, 960, 1088\}$ adds a further +2.0. In output space, a consensus-driven vote fusion clusters candidates by true mask IoU rather than box IoU and, gated by cross-view agreement, emits either the seed mask or a consensus-denoised mask per cluster. The first two operators stack near-additively (predicted 36.5 inshore, measured 36.1). The third is not an additive term but an enabler, because the same eight views collapse to 4.1 inshore under matrix non-maximum suppression (NMS) and erode offshore AP from 59.6 to 55.9 under unconditional pixelwise majority voting. Composed, the stack lifts 27.7/45.6/59.6 to 38.8/53.8/64.8 mask AP on the inshore, official, and offshore splits (+11.1/+8.2/+5.2) at zero additional training and roughly $8\times$ inference. The exchange is explicit. A training budget is spent instead as a deliberation budget, and the accuracy-compute dial that results stays in the operator's hands rather than being frozen into a set of weights.

We position the result as a cascade of three rungs and claim neither more nor less than the numbers support. The training-free rung posts the strongest detection axis, an inshore AP$_{50}$ of 75.6 against O2Former's 69.5, although it does not lead on absolute inshore AP (38.8 against 43.1) or AP$_{75}$ (36.1 against 45.9); the stack is a detection-and-recall recovery, not a boundary-precision method. What the two remaining rungs share is their supervision. A deployed model makes its mistakes on the training split as readily as on the test split, and those mistakes are free supervision: we train nothing but the corrector, and no new annotation is drawn.

The second rung supplies exactly the missing ingredient as a post-processor in the boundary-patch refinement (BPR) paradigm \cite{bpr2021}. A 2.35M-parameter UNet, trained in about 19 min on train-split boundary patches, edits masks only within a $\pm 4$-pixel band around predicted boundaries and never touches the main model or any detection score. With this refiner, inshore reaches 43.6 AP and 47.0 AP$_{75}$ under the protocol caveats stated in Section~IV. The third rung closes the ranking axis. Mask AP is a ranking metric, and a candidate whose mask is accurate but whose score sits below a worse neighbor forfeits precision at every recall level it should have dominated; the fusion stage already computes the quantity that predicts mask quality, namely the cross-view consensus of each cluster, yet passes the seed score through unchanged, because no hand-written function of that quantity helped all three splits at once. We therefore learn the function rather than write it down. A gradient-boosted regression tree over 14 scalar features, fitted in 19 s on the base model's own training-split errors, re-ranks detections without editing a single mask pixel and carries the pipeline to 44.5 inshore AP and 48.3 inshore AP$_{75}$, above the 500-epoch O2Former on both metrics at 55 epochs of training, with 59.4 official and 70.1 offshore, where that architecture still leads by 1.6. The training-free claim is accordingly scoped to the recovery stack, and both correctors are reported separately throughout. An equal-epoch control reinforces this framing: extending training to 200 epochs and applying the same recovery yields 37.9/52.5/63.5, below the short-schedule configuration. The main contributions of this article are summarized as follows.
\begin{itemize}
\item A pretraining-regime study for SAR ship instance segmentation. COCO initialization alone moves plain Mask2Former from 17.9 to 27.7 inshore mask AP, and a controlled seven-direction ablation shows that every architecture- or training-side modification of the strong base was flat, negative, or absorbed, a pattern we term the preservation regularity.
\item A training-free three-operator recovery framework, composed of a same-basin weight soup, wide multi-scale flip TTA, and consensus-vote fusion, that lifts inshore mask AP by +11.1 (+40\% relative) at zero additional training and roughly $8\times$ inference cost. Transferred to PSeg-SSDD, the same stack exceeds six comparison methods that we trained ourselves under one protocol, drawn from the two-stage, single-stage prototype, dynamic-kernel, and SAR-specific module families, on all three splits.
\item Evidence that the fusion operator, not the view count, decides whether aggressive TTA helps or harms. Under identical views, matrix NMS collapses to 4.1 inshore and majority voting erodes offshore AP below the no-TTA base, whereas the consensus vote matches or exceeds the seed variant on all three splits.
\item A negative-results ledger reported under the same controls as the positives, including the seven-direction sweep and a 200-epoch equal-budget null, which rules out capacity as the recoverable lever in the forms tested.
\item A three-rung self-correcting inference cascade that adds two small trained correctors on top of the training-free stack, each fitted on the base model's own errors on the training split and neither requiring a new annotation. The first is a 2.35M-parameter boundary-patch refiner, trained in about 19 min and confined to a $\pm 4$-pixel boundary band, which converts the stack's known boundary imprecision into accuracy (inshore AP$_{75}$ from 36.1 to 47.0). The second is a 14-feature gradient-boosted consensus re-ranker, fitted in 19 s on statistics the fusion stage already produces, which edits detection scores only and leaves every mask pixel bit-identical, carrying the pipeline to 44.5 inshore AP and 48.3 inshore AP$_{75}$, above the strongest published 500-epoch architecture on both metrics. The refiner is audited along seven ground-truth-leakage vectors and the re-ranker under a separate adversarial protocol, and applying the recipe to six further carriers spanning five architecture families across two datasets leaves every carrier above its own baseline on all three splits.
\end{itemize}
