\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{A Preservation Regularity: When Architecture Engineering Pays}
\label{sec:disc-preservation}

A single regularity organizes the controlled ledger of Section~IV: on a strong COCO-pretrained base, a modification survives in proportion to how much of the pretrained representation it preserves. Every weight-touching intervention we tested failed to pay. Constraining the deformable-attention sampling offsets cost 1.0 inshore point, one-to-many auxiliary supervision was flat, the four training-side families (capacity, feature enrichment, data augmentation, supervision) were each subsumed by the COCO-initialized base, the MaskIoU rescoring head (worth +1.5 inshore standalone) left a +0.1 residual under the weight soup, the zero-gated strip adapter cost 0.9, and the Swin-S backbone swap gained +0.9 at the base yet ended at 37.3/52.5/63.5 under the full recovery stack, below the ResNet-50 configuration on all three splits. The 200-epoch equal-budget control repeated the pattern on the schedule axis, reaching 37.9/52.5/63.5. Meanwhile, the three operators that left the weights untouched delivered the entire +11.1/+8.2/+5.2 lift.

The regularity is regime-dependent, not universal. On a from-scratch Mask2Former at 17.9 inshore mask AP, the specialized modules of O2Former are worth +25.2 points \cite{o2former2025}; in our own earlier experiments, a Gaussian--Wasserstein matching cost gained +4.4 inshore (12.5 to 16.9) on an ImageNet-backbone, from-scratch-head base, yet turned neutral to negative (27.9 to 27.4) once the identical model was COCO-initialized. Same mechanism, two regimes, opposite signs. Architecture engineering therefore pays where the representation is weak enough to leave headroom for injected priors, and stops paying once COCO initialization has already supplied what those priors encode. The adaptation literature corroborates this reading from outside SAR: Mona reports that a convolutional adapter preserving the pretrained representation surpasses full fine-tuning by +0.9 mask AP on COCO, whereas the linear LoRand adapter falls 0.4 below it \cite{mona2025}. Preservation is necessary but not sufficient. Our strip adapter respected preservation exactly, through a scalar zero-initialized residual gate that reproduces the COCO model at the start of fine-tuning, and still lost 0.9 inshore point because the elongation prior mismatches the quasi-point-like near-shore hulls of HRSID. The optional boundary-patch refiner is consistent with, not a counterexample to, the same regularity: its 2.35M parameters sit beside the frozen pipeline rather than inside it, the pretrained weight alignment is untouched, and its gain lands precisely on the strict-IoU boundary axis that no weight-touching change bought. The learned re-ranker obeys the regularity even more literally, since it never reads a pixel and never edits a mask, and acts only on the ordering of candidates the frozen pipeline has already produced.

\subsection{What Did Not Help, and Why It Matters}
\label{sec:disc-negatives}

Several directions that should have worked did not, and each failure marks a boundary of the error-axis decomposition rather than a gap in the engineering. The most instructive is speckle marginalization. Multiplicative speckle is the one perturbation that SAR imaging physics specifies exactly, so re-drawing it and adjudicating the resulting decisions is a physically principled fourth view axis, and we archived eight falsifiable predictions before the first GPU run of that arm. Two variants were built and both lost. The first estimates the backscatter field by median despeckling with a bright-scatterer pass-through and re-draws Gamma speckle on that estimate; its best fusion reached 37.1 inshore against 38.8 for the geometric stack and was negative on the other two splits as well, 51.7 official and 62.3 offshore against 53.8 and 64.8, while its base-free majority vote fell to 27.0, below the 29.6 of plain single-view inference, which convicts the despeckling estimator of erasing 10- to 30-pixel hulls. The second variant drops that estimator and multiplies mild Gamma draws onto the image directly; its base-free vote recovers to 30.0, clearing the estimator of blame, yet the fused result still stops at 37.3. The reason is subset absorption. Multi-scale inference already resamples every chip between 640 and 1088 pixels, which alters the effective speckle correlation, so the decisions a speckle re-draw can flip lie inside the set the geometric views flip. The gain theorem survives untouched; what does not survive is its empirical premise that an unharvested speckle reserve exists on a base trained with heavy scale jitter. One prediction did survive, and was confirmed twice: fusion that keeps the base prediction as an arbiter beat pure $K$-draw majority voting in both variants, 37.1 against 27.0 and 37.3 against 30.0, exactly as the negative-margin analysis warned that marginalization without an arbiter would freeze confident errors in place. A theory that predicts the shape of its own failure is worth more than one that predicts only successes.

The architecture side closed the same way. A minimal-footprint variant of the base improved every split of both datasets, reaching 28.2/46.3/60.5 on HRSID against 27.7/45.6/59.6 for the audited base, and then surrendered the entire improvement the moment the stack was applied to it: 36.4/51.6/63.0 against 38.8/53.8/64.8 for the unmodified base under the identical eight-view protocol, carrying the single-seed, soup-free caveat of the Swin-S arm. A stem lateral connection cost 6.9 AP$_{75}$ points outright, and a gentler resampling variant, positive in aggregate, still gave up 1.0 inshore AP$_{75}$, the very axis it was built to serve. Together with the Swin-S and 200-epoch controls, this is the direct form of the interchangeability claim: a training-side gain and an inference-side gain that act on the same detection-variance axis substitute for one another instead of adding, because the stack has already driven that axis toward its floor and a better-trained base merely reaches the same floor from below. Two further nulls are of a different kind. A miss-rescue verifier that re-scores the below-threshold reject pool moved the three splits by at most 0.03 AP, because the training-free stack has already lifted every recoverable ship out of that pool. A 16-feature re-ranker, built by adding decoder-depth consistency to the 14 consensus and geometry statistics, tied with the smaller model on all three splits, 44.5/59.3/70.0 against 44.5/59.4/70.1, because stability across decoder depth and agreement across views report the same evidence twice. Read as a ledger of failures this is discouraging. Read as a map it is not: every axis we could name has exactly one remedy that moves it, and the directions that fail are the ones duplicating a remedy already in place.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:disc-limitations}

Four limitations bound our claims. First, single-look amplitude imagery imposes a sensor ceiling. The physics that separates a berthed hull from the dock structure it touches, including azimuth sub-aperture anisotropy and polarimetric signatures, is absent from the input, and a radiometric-consistency re-ranking probe recovered +0.0 inshore over a flip-TTA control, consistent with, though not proof of, a modality-bounded residual. Second, the residual gap has moved rather than closed. The near-shore split of HRSID is now ours by 1.4 AP and 2.4 AP$_{75}$, 44.5 and 48.3 against 43.1 and 45.9 for O2Former \cite{o2former2025}, so the strict-overlap axis on which specialized architectures held their near-shore advantage is no longer a deficit for us; offshore, however, still trails by 1.6 points (70.1 versus 71.7, and 70.9 for DiffSARShipInst \cite{diffsar2025}), the PSeg-SSDD inshore split remains 1.1 below the same architecture (62.1 versus 63.2) and its offshore split 2.1 below (69.8 versus 71.9), and the training-free tier alone does not lead on absolute inshore AP (38.8 versus 43.1). Third, the evidence base is single-GPU and two-dataset: the recipe transfers from HRSID to PSeg-SSDD with every operator's sign preserved (Section~\ref{sec:exp:ssdd}) and leaves five frozen carriers we did not design above their own baselines on every split, but both datasets are single-sensor amplitude collections, and the architecture- and training-side sweep is eliminative rather than exhaustive, so stronger interventions than the forms we tested may exist. Fourth, two of the three tiers are trained, so every training-free claim is scoped to the recovery stack of Section~III. The boundary-patch refiner (2.35M parameters, 19 minutes) and the consensus re-ranker (14 scalar features, 19 seconds) are reported as separate tiers in every table, and each is refitted on the training split of the dataset it is applied to, which makes their cross-dataset transfer a re-fit rather than a zero-shot carry-over; the refined-tier comparison also inherits the protocol caveat of Section~IV, where 55 COCO-initialized epochs and 500 from-scratch epochs are different operating points. Within these bounds, the central results stand on same-ground-truth controlled comparisons: a recovery of +16.8/+13.8/+10.5 points over the audited base, an inshore AP$_{50}$ of 75.6 from the training-free tier alone against 69.5 for the strongest published architecture, and near-shore AP and AP$_{75}$ above that architecture at roughly a tenth of its training epochs.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This article diagnosed the near-shore residual of a strong COCO-pretrained Mask2Former on HRSID as a variance problem rather than a capacity problem, with the pretraining regime itself the largest single lever: 17.9 versus 27.7 inshore mask AP for the identical architecture. We introduced a training-free recovery stack, composed of a same-basin weight soup, wide multi-scale flip test-time augmentation, and consensus-driven mask-IoU vote fusion, which lifted the base from 27.7/45.6/59.6 to 38.8/53.8/64.8 inshore/official/offshore mask AP at zero additional training and roughly $8\times$ inference cost. On top of that stack we devised two small correctors, each aimed at one unsaturated error axis and each supervised by nothing but the frozen model's own mistakes on the training split: a 2.35M-parameter boundary-patch refiner, band-guarded to a $\pm 4$-pixel strip around predicted contours and audited along seven leakage vectors, and a gradient-boosted re-ranker over 14 consensus and geometry statistics that reorders detections after 19 seconds of fitting without altering a single mask pixel. The three-tier cascade reached 44.5/59.4/70.1 mask AP with an inshore AP$_{75}$ of 48.3, placing near-shore accuracy 1.4 AP and 2.4 AP$_{75}$ above the strongest specialized 500-epoch architecture at roughly a tenth of its training epochs. Controlled negatives carried the diagnosis. Architecture- and training-side directions on the strong base were flat, negative, or absorbed by the stack that followed them, a 200-epoch equal-budget control did not close the gap, speckle marginalization failed in the manner its own theory had predicted, and the fusion ablation showed that the merge operator, not the view count, decides whether aggressive test-time augmentation helps or collapses. Universality was measured rather than asserted. On five frozen carriers we did not design, spanning two-stage region-based, cascade, dynamic-kernel, prototype-based, and grid-based mask-kernel designs, across both datasets and all three splits, every carrier ended above its own baseline, the training-free and boundary tiers proved to depend on carrier strength with opposite signs, and Cascade R-CNN carrying the recipe reached 42.3 inshore mask AP, within 0.8 point of an architecture purpose-built for this task. Transferred to PSeg-SSDD with no change beyond a proportional rescaling of the augmentation scales, the complete recipe took our own base from 51.9/60.9/64.8 to 62.1/67.5/69.8. In the future, we plan to extend the recipe to sensors providing polarimetric or sub-aperture information, where the amplitude ceiling identified here may be broken. What an operator receives from this work is not a stronger model but a setting: on strong pretrained bases the frontier has left the weights, and what remains is to decide how much deliberation each image deserves.
